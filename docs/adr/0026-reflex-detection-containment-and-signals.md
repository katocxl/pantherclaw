# ADR-0026 — Reflex: detection over the ledger, containment in the decision path, Shared Signals

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-15; G0 M10 founder decisions F1–F4, answered with the recommended option; accepted with the [G0 M10 brief](../g0/M10.md))

**Context.** By M11.1 PantherClaw decides every governed agent action and records it in the evidence ledger, but nothing reads those records for attacks, and the only broad response is the org-wide kill switch. M10 adds detection, investigation and response (Reflex). Three questions shape it. Where does detection run, given that the decision path must stay fast and fail closed (HR-001) and an attacker who can slow detection must not be able to slow or open decisions? How do responses narrower than the kill switch take effect, and how does a responder know they actually took effect on every path an agent could use (T-108)? And how far can the customer's IdP, through Shared Signals (ADR-0019), change authority, given that the endpoint is reachable by anyone on the network (T-113)?

**Decision.**
1. **Detection runs over the evidence ledger, off the decision path** (HR-240, HR-241, HR-243). A per-org background job reads the ledger in chain order (`ledger_chain.seq`) through a normalizing feed, evaluates CEL rules with windows, first-seen sets and short sequences, and raises signals in the same transaction that advances its cursor by compare-and-set, so every entry is evaluated once per org. The decision pipeline never waits for detection and never reads its results; an overloaded, misconfigured or disabled detector costs detection, never a decision. Cost is bounded by CEL limits, batch sizes and cardinality caps whose overflow is itself reported as an owned failure, so flooding is visible rather than silent (T-105). The built-in pack ships inside the release-signed binary and is digest-checked at startup; custom rules (Business) publish only with passing firing and non-firing tests, raise signals only (never act), and run only while their digest matches the ledger-recorded one. Alert and incident history is append-only and every alert keeps its rule digest, pack version and basis, so observation and inference stay apart (F648).
2. **Containment controls are enforced in the decision path** (HR-248, HR-249, HR-252). Graduated controls (DENY_ALL, HOLD_WRITES, DENY_OPERATION) on a run, agent, grant with its dependants, connection, resource or principal are rows the Authority reads in the decision transaction, alongside the kill switch. Adding or lifting a control bumps the containment epoch in the same transaction (HR-002), so gateways learn of it through the existing containment stream and refuse permits from older epochs. A response action is CONFIRMED only when every material path is: a gateway path when that gateway acknowledges the new epoch; a bypass route (OBSERVE_ONLY or UNKNOWN coverage) only when a credential revoke or rotation closes it; otherwise the action is PARTIAL with the unresolved paths listed. New access stopped is reported apart from historical exposure (F561). Wider controls need the caller to have seen a preview of their consequences (a matching preview digest), and lifting any control is a restoration with an independent reviewer and recorded preconditions, never a delete (HR-250).
3. **The Shared Signals receiver trusts configured transmitters, and only opens cases by default** (HR-261, HR-262). The endpoint accepts RFC 8935 push SETs only from transmitters a person configured and activated with a security-key step-up. Keys come only from the JWKS pinned to the issuer's host, fetched through the egress client; header-supplied keys (`jku`, `x5u`, `jwk`) are refused; only RS256 and ES256 verify; `iss`, `aud`, a ±5-minute `iat` and a per-transmitter `jti` are checked; bodies and rates are bounded. A verified event about a known person opens a case. Containment from an IdP event happens only through the identity playbook, which is off by default, is enabled or changed only by two distinct people with distinct security keys, and acts only through ordinary containment controls that restoration lifts. The IdP is therefore trusted to *report* identity events, not to *grant or keep* authority, and a compromised IdP can at worst cause reversible containment where the customer opted in (R-30).
4. **Outbound export is an at-least-once, signed, allow-listed feed** (HR-259, HR-260). OCSF 1.9 batches go to HTTPS destinations a person activated with a step-up, through the egress client's SSRF checks, filtered by per-destination data classes and signed with Standard Webhooks; each event's `metadata.uid` is its ledger entry id, so consumers dedupe (R-31).

**Consequences.**
- Detection latency is the chain interval plus the job interval (seconds), not zero; the decision path's latency and failure modes are unchanged by detection.
- The decision transaction reads one more indexed table (active containment controls); the epoch bump on every add or lift makes containment as fast as the kill switch on gateways.
- New records: feed cursors, rules and their state, alerts, incidents, suppressions, owned failures, cases and entries, containment controls, response actions and paths, exposure snapshots, posture findings, saved queries, metric samples, export destinations and cursors, transmitters, received SETs and the identity playbook (migrations 00120–00128).
- Detections are heuristics (R-28); credential reach is only as complete as credential metadata (R-29); a compromised IdP can open cases or, with the playbook on, cause reversible containment (R-30); export and the live stream can deliver an event twice (R-31).
- Responders get an honest PARTIAL instead of a false "contained" whenever a bypass route exists, which pushes customers towards custody and enforced coverage.

**Alternatives considered.**
- Detection inline in the decision pipeline (evaluate rules before answering): rejected. It adds latency and failure modes to every decision, lets a noisy or adversarial rule slow authorization, and turns detection into a second policy engine; policy and sequences already decide in line.
- A separate event bus (Kafka, NATS) or a streaming engine: rejected. The ledger already orders every event per org with a gap-free sequence and lives in Postgres (ADR-0003, ADR-0009); River jobs (ADR-0006) are enough at this scale and add no dependency.
- Detection rules that act (auto-containment from any rule): rejected for custom rules (F4). Built-in containment comes from the identity playbook and SOAR, both explicit and reversible; a rule only raises a signal.
- Containment as more grant revocations: rejected. Revocation is not reversible as a set, cannot hold writes while allowing reads, and does not cover principals or resources; controls are separate rows lifted through restoration.
- Reporting containment CONFIRMED when the control row is written: rejected. A gateway that has not acknowledged the epoch or a route that bypasses every gateway still allows access; per-path confirmation shows it.
- IdP events that suspend grants directly (no playbook, or a one-person switch): rejected by the founder (F1). The IdP is outside the trust boundary; containment from it needs a two-person, security-key opt-in.
- Fetching the transmitter's keys from the SET (`jku`) or any URL: rejected. Keys come only from the JWKS pinned when a person configured the transmitter.
- A separate offline signing root for detection content: rejected (G0 M10 D12). The pack ships in the release-signed binary and is digest-checked at startup; custom rules are tied to their ledger-recorded digest.
