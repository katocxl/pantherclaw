# ADR-0027 — Governed automations: execution identity, ceiling-bounded issuance, closed steps, signed inbound events

**Status:** Proposed (2026-10-15; G0 M11 part 2 founder decisions F1–F6, answered with the recommended option; accepted with the [G0 M11 part 2 brief](../g0/M11.md))

**Context.** Until M11 every grant comes from a person (G0 M4 decision 7, HR-161), and nothing in PantherClaw starts work by itself. Customers want work that starts on a schedule, on a platform event or on a ticket from their own systems: launch an agent per ticket, revoke a grant when a task closes, chase an approval, deliver a digest. Done outside PantherClaw, that work runs under a long-lived credential with standing authority and no record of why it started. Four questions shape M11's answer. Whose identity does an automation act under, so that it is never a hidden administrator (F025)? How can an automation give an agent authority without becoming a machine that mints authority if it is compromised (T-120)? What may an automation do, given that the server must never become a second, unmediated path to tools (F3)? And how can systems outside PantherClaw start work, given that the endpoint is reachable by anyone on the network (T-122)?

**Decision.**
1. **Each automation acts as its own execution identity** (HR-270, HR-274). Creating an automation creates a service account linked to it, immutably. That account can hold no API keys, assertion keys or role bindings, cannot be edited or re-enabled through the service-account API, and no request can authenticate as it; database triggers back this up. The engine acts as that account only through dedicated in-process functions, never through RPC handlers or role checks. Every grant, run, launch, case, containment action, delivery and audit record an automation causes carries the automation, version, execution and trigger, and automations appear in agent inventory and exposure like agents.
2. **Grants come from a ceiling that people approved, issued fresh per execution** (F1, HR-271, HR-272, HR-273; amends G0 M4 decision 7). A version that launches agents declares an issuance ceiling: the agents, the operations and resources, requirements, per-grant limits (TTL at most 24 hours, budgets, counters) and per-day caps on grants, runs and budget. Enabling the version, and with it the ceiling, is a BINDING security-key ceremony over its digest by people who each hold `automation.publish` and every permission the version's steps need at every scope they touch (for launching: `grant.issue` and `run.start` on each ceiling agent's path); two distinct people with distinct keys when the org has two such people. The ceiling expires at a review date at most 90 days ahead. Each execution then gets a fresh grant from the engine, in one transaction with the same envelope, principal, promotion and cap checks as a person's issuance, bounded by the ceiling and counted against the per-day caps by conditional increments. Before every start and every effectful step the engine re-checks the owner, the enabled version, the ceiling and each approver's current permissions; an approver losing a permission voids the ceiling and pauses the automation. The `Issue` RPC stays human only, and the database refuses a service-account root grant without a ceiling. G0 M4 decision 7 ("only people issue grants until M11") is amended to: people issue grants through the API; automations issue grants only through the engine, inside an ACTIVE ceiling that people approved; agents and ordinary service accounts never issue grants.
3. **Steps come from a closed catalogue, and tool calls go only through enrolled agents** (F3, HR-279, HR-284, HR-285, HR-286). The catalogue is a fixed list of step kinds (launch a run, wait for it or its effect, a human gate, revoke, notify, deliver, contain, open a case, and the security-work steps), each with a schema, an edition, an effect class, a retry-safety flag and the permission its confirmers need. A version may use only kinds and scopes it declares. The server never calls a tool: work on targets happens only in runs of enrolled agents, either the customer's own or the PantherClaw runner agent (`pclaw automation runner`), an ordinary enrolled workload whose calls the version declares and the gateway decides, holds and receipts like any agent action. Automations never publish, activate, promote, approve or confirm anything; what they make for people (owner proposals, baselines, restrictions) stays a draft or a proposal.
4. **Outside systems start work only through signed inbound events** (F2, HR-275, HR-276, HR-277, HR-278). An admin adds each event source; it stays PENDING until a person activates it with a security-key step-up. Requests carry a Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 signature with the source's secret (a 24-hour overlap on rotation), a timestamp within five minutes and a `webhook-id` unseen for seven days; bodies are at most 64 KiB, rate-limited per IP and per source, and never stored. An event's fields fill only the declared, typed, bounded parameters of a version, and only in fields marked templatable; they never choose the identity, principal, agents, ceiling, operations, amounts, destinations, step kinds or trigger. One execution starts per dedupe key, trigger ancestry refuses loops, and hourly caps refuse floods with one grouped issue.

**Consequences.**
- An automation is visible, attributable and bounded: its reach is the ceiling, shown separately from its current execution reach (HR-289), and quiet schedules are never ranked as low risk.
- A compromised automation, runner agent or leaked source secret can at worst use grants inside an approved ceiling, capped per grant and per day, for at most 24 hours each (R-33, R-34); two colluding approvers remain a residual risk (R-35).
- Revoking the ceiling revokes every grant issued under it; the kill switch pauses every automation and cancels executions, and restore resumes none (HR-283).
- No new dependency: the ceremony reuses M11.1's BINDING construction, signing reuses Standard Webhooks, schedules use an in-house cron parser with embedded `time/tzdata`, and execution runs on River.
- New records: automations, versions, ceilings and usage, change requests, triggers and their log, schedules, sources and receipts, destinations and deliveries, executions, step attempts and launches, proposals, tests and simulations (migrations 00140–00147).
- Customers who need branching workflows wait for the full engine (Next); M11 definitions are linear steps with guards, gates and waits.

**Alternatives considered.**
- Service accounts holding `grant.issue` issue grants directly (G0 M4 decision 7's alternative): rejected. A compromised automation could mint any authority its account allows, with no per-execution bound and no review date.
- A standing grant per automation, reused by every execution: rejected. It is authority at rest between executions and cannot be bounded per task.
- Automations acting as their owner: rejected. It hides automated work behind a person (F025) and breaks when the owner leaves.
- A server-side tool dispatcher: rejected (F3). It would be a second path to targets beside the gateway, holding credentials outside custody and without per-action receipts.
- An open-ended scripting step (code or arbitrary HTTP from the server): rejected. Nothing could review what a version does, and the catalogue's per-kind permissions would mean nothing.
- Unsigned or shared-secret webhooks, or event fields that select agents or amounts: rejected (F2). Anyone who can reach the endpoint could start work or widen it.
- Re-checking authority only at enable time: rejected. Approvers lose permissions and leave; the check runs before every start and every effectful step.
