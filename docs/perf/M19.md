# M19 decision path: one retry of a busy budget

**Date:** 2026-10-30. **Measured by:** Claude Code. **Target:** PostgreSQL through the Authority, no gateway and no real money.

M19 adds one jittered retry (5–25 ms) of a busy budget row inside `finalize.Authorize`, from a fresh snapshot (HR-505). It does not move Prepare into the decision snapshot: snapshots stay `REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY` (HR-504). So this page answers three questions: what the retry costs and buys, whether the path without a busy row moved, and what the first refund of a budget period costs.

## Summary

| Three rounds side by side: median of rounds, max is the highest of any round | Before (`fdd50b6d`) | After (`feat/m19-speed`) |
|---|---|---|
| Refund with the row held 150 ms: allowed | 5 of 90 | **90 of 90** |
| Answer with the row held 150 ms, p50 / max | 120.1 / 184.4 ms | 157.8 / 227.2 ms |
| Answer with the row held 500 ms (still `BUDGET_BUSY`), p50 / max | 124.5 / 177.5 ms | 259.7 / 328.9 ms |
| Refund, 1 worker, Authorize p50 / p99 | 20.2 / 63.7 ms (2 rounds) | 22.1 / 69.0 ms |
| Refund, 8 workers, Authorize p50 / p99 | 82.5 / 237.5 ms | 81.9 / 287.0 ms |
| Allowed read of a `pii` field (records `read_class`), p50 / p99 | 14.0 / 56.0 ms | 14.0 / 51.9 ms |
| First refund of a period: extra Authorize cost, paired p50 | 4.4 ms | 5.2 ms |

- **The retry turns a short hold into an answer.** A row held for 150 ms, just past the 100 ms lock timeout, was `BUDGET_BUSY` nearly every time before and was allowed every time after.
- **Worst-case busy latency is about 2×100 ms plus at most 25 ms,** on top of the two evaluations. A row held past both attempts answered `BUDGET_BUSY` at a p50 of 260 ms and at most 329 ms here, against 125 ms and 178 ms before.
- **The p50 without a busy row did not move.** Refund p50 stayed within 1.6 ms of before in every paired round where both finished, and read p50 within 0.8 ms.
- **The refund p99 was higher after in every paired round, and this page does not explain it.** The gap is +2.0 and +4.7 ms with 1 worker and +8.9, +49.5 and +17.3 ms with 8 workers. It could be retries in the tail or noise, since a p99 of 1,000 refunds is the tenth-slowest one. The benchmark does not count retries, so these runs cannot tell the two apart.
- **First-of-period Prepare costs about 5 ms, once per budget period.** That is about one more transaction on a 17–22 ms Authorize, so it stays in its own transaction (D208).
- **Absolute numbers are not SLO evidence.** PN-011.1's p99 is a target, not a gate, and this shared laptop does not meet it. Only the paired differences mean anything here.

## Machine and setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Laptop | Intel Core i7-11370H (4 cores, 8 threads), Windows 11 Home 10.0.26300, the same machine as M6 |
| Shared load | Other Claude Code sessions were building and testing at the same time. No other integration, range or end-to-end run overlapped (the fleet's heavy lock was held). |
| PostgreSQL | One private `postgres:17-bookworm` container per variant, default settings |
| Client | The integration test binary, static Linux, in the database container's network namespace, so the round trips are loopback |
| Before | `fdd50b6d` (`main` with M16 and M17) plus only `bench_m19_integration_test.go` copied in |
| After | `268f3bff` (the same `main` merged into this branch) plus that file |

**Method.** Before and after started at the same moment in each of three rounds, per the side-by-side rule of the earlier perf pages: sequential runs on this laptop drift up to 5×. Each benchmark runs as its own process on a fresh database.

```bash
go test -tags integration -run '^$' -bench '^BenchmarkRefund$' -benchtime 1000x ./internal/authority/adapters/pgauthority/
go test -tags integration -run '^$' -bench '^BenchmarkM19FirstOfPeriod$' -benchtime 200x ./internal/authority/adapters/pgauthority/
go test -tags integration -run '^$' -bench '^BenchmarkM19BusyRow$' -benchtime 30x ./internal/authority/adapters/pgauthority/
go test -tags integration -run '^$' -bench '^BenchmarkM19Read$' -benchtime 500x ./internal/authority/adapters/pgauthority/
```

M16 and M17 are in both variants. `BenchmarkM19Read` carries the write of `read_class` (HR-430). A refund is not an external disclosure unless its route says so, so no claim is made here that `BenchmarkRefund` reads the history.

## Results (ms)

### A busy row

`BenchmarkM19BusyRow`: another transaction holds the budget row, a refund arrives, and the hold ends after the time shown. 30 refunds per round, lock timeout 100 ms.

| Hold | Round | Allowed, before | Allowed, after | Answer p50, before | Answer p50, after | Answer max, before | Answer max, after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 150 ms | 1 | 4 of 30 | 30 of 30 | 137.2 | 171.3 | 172.4 | 227.2 |
| 150 ms | 2 | 1 of 30 | 30 of 30 | 119.3 | 154.0 | 162.2 | 199.1 |
| 150 ms | 3 | 0 of 30 | 30 of 30 | 120.1 | 157.8 | 184.4 | 205.4 |
| 500 ms | 1 | 0 of 30 | 0 of 30 | 125.3 | 262.2 | 166.2 | 328.9 |
| 500 ms | 2 | 0 of 30 | 0 of 30 | 119.4 | 249.1 | 177.5 | 302.6 |
| 500 ms | 3 | 0 of 30 | 0 of 30 | 124.5 | 259.7 | 170.2 | 327.8 |

A 500 ms hold outlasts both attempts, so it shows the most a request waits before `BUDGET_BUSY`: two lock timeouts, the jitter, and two evaluations.

### No busy row

`BenchmarkRefund`: 1,000 refunds on one budget, each Authorize, BeginDispatch and RecordExecution. Authorize p50 / p99.

| Workers | Round | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | failed: `BUDGET_BUSY` | 23.32 / 73.17 |
| 1 | 2 | 21.78 / 64.37 | 22.10 / 69.04 |
| 1 | 3 | 18.53 / 62.98 | 18.37 / 64.97 |
| 8 | 1 | 132.7 / 440.9 | 131.1 / 449.8 |
| 8 | 2 | 74.55 / 237.5 | 75.13 / 287.0 |
| 8 | 3 | 82.54 / 228.4 | 81.95 / 245.7 |

The failed run stopped at one refund that answered `CANNOT_AUTHORIZE` `BUDGET_BUSY`, because the benchmark treats any refund without a permit as an error. It happened once, with one worker, on the "before" binary. One such event proves little on its own, but it is the failure the retry exists for. Refunds per second matched within 2% in every paired round.

`BenchmarkM19Read`: 500 allowed reads of a `pii` field. Authorize p50 / p99.

| 1 | 17.36 / 61.34 | 18.10 / 66.35 |

| 3 | 13.96 / 52.21 | 13.99 / 51.85 |

### First refund of a budget period

`BenchmarkM19FirstOfPeriod`: 200 fresh budgets. On each, the first refund finds no row and makes Prepare's transaction, and the next refund finds the row. The extra cost is the median of the paired differences.

| Round | First p50 / p99, after | Next p50 / p99, after | Extra cost p50, before | Extra cost p50, after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 28.57 / 89.28 | 22.49 / 86.13 | 6.58 | 5.57 |
| 2 | 24.10 / 63.37 | 18.22 / 56.67 | 4.04 | 5.20 |
| 3 | 21.05 / 80.79 | 17.13 / 59.23 | 4.42 | 4.27 |

## Not measured

- The benchmark does not count how many decisions took the retry on a contended budget.
- The gateway and the network are not in these numbers. Authorize runs in-process against loopback PostgreSQL.
- The staging numbers are pending, as on [M13](M13.md).
