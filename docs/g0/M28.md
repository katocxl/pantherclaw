### G0 — M28 — Threat map: MITRE ATLAS and ATT&CK Enterprise, lanes from evidence, signed report and exports — 2026-11-03

This brief is written before implementation (G0). It lists planned scope; delivery status is tracked by the M28 pull requests and, at the end of the milestone, in the exit status section. The design was written from a reading of the M27 exit branch and the brief on `main` at 86d7653b. The founder approved the plan as planned on 2026-11-03 (Q1–Q4) and answered two further questions the same day. The streams build in parallel and land as one exit pull request.

**Purpose:** a security team thinks in MITRE ATT&CK and ATLAS tactics and wants to know, per technique, what PantherClaw does about it and what evidence there is. M28 adds a threat map: every technique of both matrices, four lanes (Prevent, Detect, Respond, Test) from PantherClaw's own records, a reason and a fix on every gap, labels on signals and exports, a signed report, an ATT&CK Navigator layer, a CLI, an MCP tool and daily history. It has no score and makes no claim about how well an organization is defended. A reading of the code found: no ATT&CK id anywhere; ATLAS ids on 5 of 20 built-in rules, checked against a hand-written list; the alert ledger body and the feed dropped `atlas` and `owasp`, so OCSF `attacks[]` was always empty; incidents and cases carried no techniques; no per-rule record of when input last arrived; and `OWASP_CWE_MAPPING.md` §6 named a wrong path and an export that did not happen.

**Design decisions:**

*S28a — catalogue and mappings*

1. **Packages.** `internal/threatmap/catalog` (a pure leaf with the embedded catalogues), `mapping` (PantherClaw's own tables), `mapset` (every declared mapping in one set, with the closure test), `domain` (the pure evaluator, used by the server and by `pclaw verify` offline), `navigator`, `ports`, `app` and adapters. Every package is BSL like its neighbours.
2. **Catalogue** (HR-682). Pins in `tools/mitre/pins.json`: ATT&CK Enterprise STIX v20.0 from `mitre-attack/attack-stix-data`, and ATLAS 2026.10, the file `dist/v6/ATLAS-2026.10.yaml` from `mitre-atlas/atlas-data`, each by exact URL and sha256. `task mitre:update` downloads, refuses a mismatch and writes `internal/threatmap/catalog/{attack-enterprise,atlas}.json` in the format `pantherclaw.mitre-catalog/v1`; it never runs in CI. The licence terms were read at pin time: NOTICE carries ATT&CK's copyright line and terms of use and the ATLAS Apache-2.0 notice, and each catalogue file carries a short notice. ATLAS tactic short names are a slug of the tactic name. Ids MITRE dropped from the pinned ATLAS release stay accepted only so stored custom rules keep parsing; PantherClaw's own content may not name them.
3. **Matrix shape.** Cells are techniques that are not sub-techniques and not deprecated or revoked, under each of their tactics in MITRE's order. A mapping may name a technique or a sub-technique; a lane fed only by sub-technique mappings is at most PARTIAL ("covers N of M sub-techniques"). An Outside entry on a technique covers its sub-techniques.
4. **Where mappings live** (HR-683, HR-685). Built-in detection rules gain `attack` beside `atlas`, or a one-line `techniques_none`; custom rules may carry ids but never `techniques_none`. Library scenarios and campaigns gain `atlas`, `attack` and a one-line `technique_why` per id, or a one-line `techniques_none` saying why they map none (used for scenarios that test PantherClaw itself), carried into `library.json` and never part of the digest; a scenario with `techniques_none` never feeds the Test lane; customer scenarios refuse all of them. Hub detection rules carry ids where a technique fits. Behaviour and content kinds, Prevent controls (each naming its refusal codes, HR rules and requirement), Outside entries (a closed list of reasons and of tool kinds) and the "No PantherClaw control yet" list are Go tables. A mapping says "this control acts on this technique when an AI agent carries it out through PantherClaw".
5. **Closure test** (HR-683). It runs in `task check` and fails unless every technique has exactly one place, every named id is current, every rule, scenario, campaign and kind is mapped or says why not, every Prevent control names a technique, an HR rule and existing refusal codes, every Hub playbook exists and every why line cites its id.
6. **`pclaw redteam run --technique ID`** runs only the library scenarios mapped to that technique or its sub-techniques. An unknown id is a usage error; no matching scenario exits 2.

*S28b — engine, API and exports ([ADR-0055](../adr/0055-mitre-threat-map.md))*

7. **Lanes, states and colour** (HR-680, HR-689). Prevent, Detect and Respond are EVIDENCED, PARTIAL, GAP, STATED or NONE_SHIPPED; Test is PASSED, FAILED, STALE or NOT_RUN; Seen is counts only ("alerts raised", "refusals by mapped controls", "holds by mapped controls"). Green when Prevent or Detect is EVIDENCED and Test is not FAILED; amber when any of Prevent, Detect or Respond is EVIDENCED or PARTIAL; red otherwise; grey when Outside. Counts per tactic and status, no total.
8. **Prevent.** A mapped control whose requirement holds, and the routes in scope from the coverage graph: every route ENFORCED within its TTL is EVIDENCED, some is PARTIAL, none is GAP. An active agent with no record counts as one unknown route.
9. **Detect.** Per mapped PantherClaw rule or kind: not licensed is PARTIAL, disabled is GAP, suppressed org-wide is PARTIAL, silent is PARTIAL, otherwise EVIDENCED. A rule is live when its input arrived in the last 7 days, recorded per rule in `pc.detection_rule_state.last_input_at` by the engine in the transaction that advances its cursor; a rule on audit kinds only is live while the engine reads the organization's events. A rule installed from the Hub counts as PantherClaw's while it is unchanged; the organization's own rules make the lane STATED and are listed as theirs.
10. **Respond.** An organization's automation counts when its trigger names a mapped PantherClaw rule in a plain `event.attrs["rule"] in [...]` or `== "..."` condition on `alert.raised` or `alert.inferred`. Active with the rule enabled and licensed is EVIDENCED (a `contain` step also needs the automation-playbooks edition, else PARTIAL); disabled, paused, or its rule off or unlicensed is PARTIAL; no automations on the edition is GAP. A Hub playbook that would fire, not installed, is a gap with the item named.
11. **Test.** The newest result per mapped library scenario with the library's digest: FAIL within 30 days is FAILED; PASS within 30 days with every step server-observed is PASSED; older counted results are STALE; else NOT_RUN. A reported pass never counts.
12. **Scope and gaps.** An agent or an environment filter, at most one; Prevent and Seen follow it, and Detect, Respond and Test are marked organization-wide. Every PARTIAL or GAP lane yields a gap with a fix from a closed list: enable a rule, end a suppression, send events, upgrade, turn on a setting, route through the gateway, enable a playbook, install a Hub item, run a scenario, or none. Labels are fixed templates.
13. **Wording** (HR-681). The statement, word for word: "This map shows the PantherClaw controls mapped to each technique and the evidence for them. A mapped control covers the technique, not every way of carrying it out. Techniques outside PantherClaw need other tools. It is not a score or a guarantee." A test checks the statement exactly, removes it, and then scans every threat-map string for the banned words.
14. **API.** A new `ThreatMapService`: `ListThreatFrameworks`, `GetThreatMatrix`, `GetThreatTechnique`, `ListThreatGaps`, `ExportNavigatorLayer` and `GetThreatMapHistory`, each under `threatmap.read`, with no side effects, classified as reads for the plan gate. Unknown or deprecated technique ids are `UNKNOWN_TECHNIQUE`.
15. **Access and editions** (HR-686). `threatmap.read` for Org Admin, Security Admin, Responder, Auditor and Investigator (a Go permission, no migration). A browser or support session reads frameworks, matrix, technique and gaps on every edition; an API key, an access token or any other credential needs Team, else `EDITION_REQUIRED` with the message "Reading the threat map with an API key or access token needs Team or higher; the console shows it on every edition." The Navigator export and the report need Team, history needs Business, for every credential. Edition checks live in the use case.
16. **History and drop alerts** (HR-687). On Business, a periodic job snapshots each organization, each environment with active agents and both frameworks once a day, keeps 400 days, and on a drop writes `audit.threatmap.lane_lost`; the built-in rule `pc.threatmap.lane_lost` raises one alert per organization, scope, framework, technique, lane and day.
17. **Exports** (HR-688). An ATT&CK Navigator layer (format 4.5, domain `enterprise-attack`, for Navigator 5.3.2) with one fixed colour and comment per technique and tactic and the statement as description, never a score, gradient or numeric metadata. MITRE's ATLAS Navigator data is deprecated and stops at an older ATLAS release, so an ATLAS export answers `NAVIGATOR_NOT_AVAILABLE`. A signed pack scope `threat_map` with `threat_map.json` (`pantherclaw.threat-map/v1`) holding every input and the mapping; `pclaw verify` re-derives it and refuses any difference, and a pack built with another catalogue reads as not available. `pclaw threats matrix|technique|gaps|export navigator|export report|history`.
18. **Posture source** `threatmap` (Team): a rule off, a playbook not installed, a playbook disabled, a failed test, one finding per fix target. Route and edition gaps stay with the coverage source and the plan page.

*S28c — signals, SIEM and MCP*

19. **Signals carry techniques** (HR-684). Alerts gain `attack`; incidents and cases gain `atlas` and `attack`, the union of their alerts' ids, capped at 64. The alert and incident ledger bodies and the feed carry `atlas`, `attack` and `owasp`: the fix that makes OCSF `attacks[]` real. OCSF fills one entry per technique and tactic with the catalogue version. Behaviour and content signals take ids from the kind table; range results from the library.
20. **SIEM packs.** `integrations/siem/rules.yaml` gains ATT&CK ids or a reason per rule, and each of the six formats carries them in its own fields; Splunk through a lookup, never an `action.*` key (HR-654). Each README states the fields.
21. **MCP** (Team). `pclaw mcp self` gains the read-only tool `technique_coverage`, calling `GetThreatTechnique`, and `search` gains a technique filter for alerts and incidents. The Investigator role gains `threatmap.read` and is admitted on the one new read, audited by the server like the others. HR-655, HR-656 and HR-657 are amended with the founder's approval on 2026-11-03.

**Two lists, never merged:**
- **Outside PantherClaw** (grey): the technique needs an endpoint, a network, a mailbox, physical access, a person, a stage before compromise, cloud infrastructure, the identity provider or model development, and names the kind of tool that covers it: EDR, network security, email security, physical security, security awareness, threat intelligence, cloud security, the identity provider or model security.
- **No PantherClaw control yet** (red): the technique is within an agent platform's reach and PantherClaw ships no control for it, with why. Every lane is NONE_SHIPPED and the fix is none.
- The entries of both lists, and the techniques by abuse case, are in [MITRE_MAPPING.md](../security/MITRE_MAPPING.md), generated from the code.
