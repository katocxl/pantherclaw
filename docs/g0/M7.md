### G0 — M7 Effects, reconciliation & proof — 2026-10-10

This brief is written before implementation (G0). It lists planned scope; delivery status is tracked by the M7 pull requests and, at the end of the milestone, in the exit status below. It builds on `main` at 1c1f3f9: M1–M4 complete, M6 through slice 22 (slices 23 and 24 wait for M5 part 2), and M5 part 2 through slice 206b. M5 part 2 is still being built in parallel; the M7 slices that need its approval binding ceremony and its `RECONCILIATION` waitlist producer are stacked on those slices (design decision 21).

**Purpose:** a decision says what was allowed, an execution receipt says what the gateway sent and what the target answered, and neither proves that the effect happened. M7 adds the third record. A verifier reads the target again, through the same gateway and credential, and states which effect it observed, at which level, on what basis and with what limits. An `UNKNOWN` outcome becomes an owned reconciliation task that keeps its budget held until evidence or an accountable person resolves it, and recovery is always a new, separately authorized action. Then M7 makes the evidence verifiable by anyone: every ledger entry goes into a per-org Merkle tree with signed checkpoints, a blinded global root is anchored in Sigstore's public Rekor v2 log with an RFC 3161 timestamp, and `pclaw verify` checks all of it offline and says exactly what it checked. Decision replay, retention, restricted payload capture and signed evidence packs complete the record. In product terms this is the Trail component, plus the reconciliation part of Checkpoint ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md) §4). The founder's eight decisions are at the end (all answered before the brief was written).

**Two tracks** (decision 8). After this brief is approved, two sessions build M7 in parallel, each on its own branches:
- **Track A, effects and reconciliation** (`internal/transactions`, the gateway's verifier runner, the payments simulator): verifiers, effect states and receipts, execution receipts kept, reconciliation and its resolution, target-log reconciliation (HR-112), compensation links, the evidence explorer.
- **Track B, proof** (`internal/evidence`, `pclaw verify`): Merkle tiles and signed checkpoints, the anchored global root, verify bundles and `pclaw verify`, decision replay, retention and legal holds, restricted payload capture, evidence packs.

**Scope (planned):**
- **Execution receipts kept** (track A). The execution receipt gains the effective action hash, the attempt, the dispatch time, who recorded it (gateway or sweeper) and the target's reference; its JWS is stored in `execution_receipts` (today only its hash reaches the ledger). The sweeper's `DISPATCHING → UNKNOWN` writes an attempt, a receipt and a ledger entry like any other outcome (today it writes nothing).
- **Verifiers** (track A, decision 1). An action definition's `verifier` gains a level, how to find the object after an accepted dispatch (`reference`) and after an unknown one (`lookup`), which observed fields must match the action (`expect`), how the target's status maps to effect states (`states`), and the limits of what it establishes. The server schedules verification tasks; the gateway serving the connection leases them over mTLS, performs the package's reviewed read with the connection's sealed credential, and reports only the declared fields.
- **Effect states and effect receipts** (track A): `CONFIRMED | NONE_CONFIRMED | PARTIAL | PROPAGATION_PENDING | CONFLICTING | UNVERIFIABLE | UNKNOWN | COMPENSATED` (ARCHITECTURE §6.2), verification levels (acceptance, follow-up read, domain effect, downstream), required versus achieved level, verifier deadlines, conflicting observations kept side by side, one signed effect receipt (`pap-effect+jwt`) per change of state, appended and never rewritten.
- **Execution states** (F471) derived for every transaction: `REQUESTED`, `BLOCKED`, `WAITING`, `AUTHORIZED`, `DISPATCHED`, `ACCEPTED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`, with `UNKNOWN` outcomes shown as dispatched with an unknown effect, never as success.
- **Reconciliation** (track A, decision 2). Every `UNKNOWN` outcome opens a reconciliation task in the same transaction, linked to M5 part 2's `RECONCILIATION` waitlist entry. Its reservations and dedupe claim stay held (HR-003, HR-007). Evidence that the effect happened (a verifier observation, or a late `accepted` report from the gateway) resolves it automatically and commits the budget. "It did not happen" releases the budget only when an independent person confirms it on a page with a security-key assertion bound to the resolution and a written basis.
- **Target-log reconciliation** (track A, HR-112): for packages that declare a target log, the gateway periodically lists the objects the target created in a time window; the server matches each to an execution receipt through the transaction id carried in the idempotency key, and reports effects without receipts.
- **Compensation and recovery links** (F502, F503, F487): a new, separately authorized transaction can be linked to an earlier one as its compensation or recovery. The link never changes the original's records or budgets, and `COMPENSATED` never claims an exact reversal.
- **Evidence explorer** (track A, F466–F468): one call returns a transaction's decision receipts (every evaluation), execution receipt, observations, effect receipts, reconciliation, links, references to the grant revision, policy version, definition, approval and connection revision, and the integrity status of each item (chained, in a checkpoint, anchored).
- **Merkle tiles and signed checkpoints** (track B, HR-111): an RFC 9162 tree per org over the chained entry hashes, stored as C2SP tlog tiles; a C2SP signed-note checkpoint per window, signed with the existing `checkpoints` key (plus an optional ML-DSA-65 co-signature, decision 6); the signer refuses a checkpoint that is not consistent with the previous one; a daily job re-verifies every org's chain against its latest checkpoint.
- **The anchored global root** (track B, decision 3): hourly, a Merkle tree over one blinded leaf per org (its latest checkpoint), signed with a new ECDSA P-256 `anchors` key, entered in a configured Rekor v2 log as a `hashedrekord` and timestamped by a configured RFC 3161 timestamp authority. Off by default; Team edition (PN-007.3).
- **`pclaw verify`** (track B, PN-007.4): offline verification of a bundle (receipts, ledger entries, inclusion and consistency proofs, checkpoints, anchors) against keys the user pinned, with a report that lists every check as passed, failed or not available, and states the limits of what passed.
- **Decision replay** (track B, decision 5, F504–F508): each evaluation's inputs are kept as one sealed record; replay runs the decision pipeline again on them, unchanged or under a proposed policy version, explains differences, reports an incomplete replay when inputs are gone, and never writes a decision or calls a gateway.
- **Retention and legal holds** (track B, decision 4, F512, F518–F521): retention per category; expired bodies are removed by a dedicated database role used by one job, leaving the hashes and a tombstone, so the chain and checkpoints still verify; legal holds block removal.
- **Restricted payload capture** (track B, decision 7, F513, F516): off by default; a capture profile with a purpose and limits, created by a person; captured at the gateway after redaction, stored sealed per row, and read only through an audited read by a person holding `evidence.read_restricted`.
- **Evidence packs** (track B, F525–F532, Team): a signed manifest and content for a selected scope (transactions, a run, an agent, a time range) that includes every effect state, the versions that applied, approvals, containment, gaps and its own privacy metadata, filtered by the creator's permissions.
- **`pclaw`**: `txn list|show` (`show` is the explorer, `GetTransactionEvidence`), `reconcile list|show|occurred|verify|link`, `evidence trust|bundle|replay`, `verify`, `pack create|list|get|download`, `retention get|set`, `hold create|list|release`, `capture profile create|list|disable`, `capture read`.
- **Simulator** (track A): refunds keep their idempotency key, creation time and a status that settles after a delay; a list endpoint filtered by charge and creation time; a fault that applies the refund and then loses the response.
- **End to end**: S07 completed (the unknown refund is found, or released by a person), a lost response, an effect without a receipt, conflicting observations, tampering detected (a row edit, a deletion, re-signing without a witness), replay that never dispatches, pack completeness.

**Out of scope:** dependent-step waits on an effect state (F500: they arrive with automation steps in M11; M7 provides the effect state they will wait on); multi-target actions (ActionIR v1 has one target; M7 reports one result per declared effect kind of that target, F490's part); export destinations other than download (S3, SIEM and OCSF are M10, PN-018); incident packs (incidents are M10; the pack scope is ready for them); automation evidence (F533, M11); a TUF client for Sigstore's trust material (`pclaw verify` reads a `trusted_root.json` the user supplies; design decision 12); public checkpoint witnesses (C2SP witnessing) beyond Rekor; ML-DSA signatures on receipts (decision 6); data-subject deletion requests (M13, PN-019); automatic reversal of anything.

**Security constraints and design decisions:**
1. **Verification reads (HR-190, decision 1).**
   - The server creates a verification task when an outcome is `accepted` and the definition has a verifier (purpose `follow_up`), and when an outcome is `unknown` (purpose `reconcile`). A task names the connection, the read operation, its target and parameters, all computed by the server from the stored action, the target's reference and the verifier specification. Nothing the agent sent reaches a task except through the reviewed mapping that produced the action.
   - The gateway serving the connection claims tasks with `ClaimVerifications` (at most 16 at a time, each lease single use and valid 30 seconds, bound to the gateway and the connection). The containment stream hints that tasks are waiting; the gateway also polls every 10 seconds.
   - The gateway accepts a task only if the operation is a definition of the connection's pinned package with `access: read`, the HTTP method is `GET` or `HEAD`, and the definition is the one the write's verifier names (the server checks the same before creating the task). It builds the request from that definition's dispatch template through the M6 egress path: host pinning, URL checks, the sealed credential opened for that request only, response caps and secret-echo redaction (HR-073, HR-076).
   - The gateway reports with `ReportObservation`: the lease, the HTTP status, the response digest, and only the fields the verifier declares (`reference`, `expect`, `states`, `correlate`), read by JSON pointer from a JSON response of at most 1 MiB. The rest of the response is dropped.
   - Reads stop when the kill switch is on, when the connection is quarantined or retired, and when the gateway's containment stream is stale, exactly like dispatches (HR-010, HR-183). The task waits; a person can still resolve it.
   - The gateway is trusted for the reads it reports, as it is for the dispatches it reports (R-01). The receipt names the gateway, so a later finding can be traced.
2. **Effect states (HR-191).**
   - Levels: `acceptance` (the target's 2xx answer), `follow_up` (an independent read found the object), `domain_effect` (a read of a field that establishes the domain effect, such as a settled status), `downstream` (another system confirms; no M7 package uses it).
   - A definition declares the level its verifier reaches and may require a level (`verifier.required`). A policy may raise the requirement with a new obligation, `verify(level)`. When the required level is above what the definition's verifier can reach, or the connection cannot serve the read, step 8 returns `CANNOT_AUTHORIZE` with `VERIFIER_UNSUPPORTED` (F497).
   - From an observation: the object found with every `expect` field equal to the action and a status in `states.confirmed` → `CONFIRMED`; in `states.pending` → `PROPAGATION_PENDING`; in `states.none` → `NONE_CONFIRMED`; found with an `expect` field that differs → `CONFLICTING`; not found by reference after the target accepted → `PROPAGATION_PENDING` until the definition's `within_seconds`, then `CONFLICTING`; a listing that is complete and has no match, at least `within_seconds` after dispatch → `NONE_CONFIRMED`; a read that failed (timeout, 5xx, any 4xx except not found) is inconclusive and retried.
   - Deadlines: tasks retry with backoff (5 s, then ×3, capped at 5 minutes) until `within_seconds`. At the deadline the state is the last conclusive one, or `UNKNOWN` if none was conclusive (a status still pending counts as inconclusive and is named in the receipt). A definition without a verifier gives `UNVERIFIABLE` with the reason. Declared side effects without their own verifier are `UNVERIFIABLE`, listed per effect kind (F490's part).
   - Monitor-mode actions are verified like others (verification observes; it prevents nothing). Delegated outcomes (the hook) are `UNVERIFIABLE` unless the definition has a verifier, and stay labelled `PARTIAL`.
   - Each change of state appends a signed effect receipt with the verifier, the level required and achieved, the expected and observed values (as a digest, with the values in `observations`), the basis (observation ids or the person), the definition's `limits` text (for example "the refund object exists; it does not show that the customer received the money", F489) and the times. Earlier receipts are never changed; `CONFLICTING` keeps both sides (F484, F498).
   - An `accepted` outcome never upgrades an effect beyond `acceptance` by itself, and nothing the agent or the target's write response says is an observation.
3. **Reconciliation (HR-192, HR-003, HR-007; decision 2).**
   - `RecordExecution` with `unknown`, and the sweeper's `DISPATCHING → UNKNOWN`, each write the attempt (`recorded_by`), the execution receipt, the ledger entry and an `OPEN` reconciliation task in one transaction, and create the `reconcile` verification. The `RECONCILIATION` waitlist entry comes from M5 part 2's producer in the same transaction: until slice A11 (stacked on M5 part 2 slice 210) the task exists without one, and A11 adds the call and links the entry. The entry escalates and never resolves by itself.
   - Reservations stay `HELD` and the dedupe claim stays `HELD`, so an exact repeat stays parked with `RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED` (HR-007).
   - **Automatic, only towards "occurred":** a `CONFIRMED` (or `PROPAGATION_PENDING` with the object found) observation resolves the task `OCCURRED` (`verifier`); so does a late `RecordExecution` with `accepted` for that permit (`late_report`). Resolving commits the reservations and sets the claim `SUCCEEDED` (the repeat window applies). A late `failed` report is kept as evidence and resolves nothing.
   - **Release, only by a person:** "did not occur" is confirmed on `/reconciliations/{id}` by a person who holds `transaction.reconcile` and is not the run's launcher, its represented principal, or the agent's owner or backup owner. They write a basis (at most 2,000 characters, stored and shown as untrusted text) and make a WebAuthn assertion whose challenge is `SHA-256(JCS({reconciliation, transaction, resolution: "not_occurred", basis_sha256, evidence, expires_at}))` through M5 part 2's binding ceremony; `evidence` lists the observation ids shown on the page. The page shows the verifier's evidence (for example "no matching refund in a complete listing, 12 minutes after dispatch") or says that there is none. The release sets the task `NOT_OCCURRED`, releases the reservations and the claim, appends a `NONE_CONFIRMED` effect receipt whose basis is the person, writes `transaction.reconciliation_released` to the ledger and notifies org admins.
   - `ResolveOccurred` lets a person holding `transaction.reconcile` record "occurred" through the API or `pclaw` with a basis: it only commits, which is the safe direction.
   - A `CONFLICTING` effect on an accepted outcome opens a task of kind `conflicting_effect`. Its budget was already committed and stays committed; the resolution records which observation the person found authoritative.
4. **Compensation and recovery (HR-193, F502, F503).** `LinkTransaction` links a later transaction (its own decision, approval and budget) to an earlier one as `compensates` or `recovers`. Only `transaction.reconcile` holders link; the later transaction must be in the same org and must have been decided after the earlier one was dispatched. The earlier transaction's effect state becomes `COMPENSATED` only when the compensating transaction's effect is `CONFIRMED`, with an appended receipt; the original records, its effect receipts and its budget are unchanged, and the receipt keeps the definition's reversibility (an irreversible effect stays "compensated, not reversed").
5. **Package format additions (HR-124).**
   - `verifier.level` (`follow_up | domain_effect`), `verifier.required` (the level the action needs; default `acceptance`).
   - `verifier.reference`: `{from_response: <JSON pointer>}` into the 2xx response of the write; the value must match the read operation's target `id_pattern`. The gateway extracts it and sends it as `target_ref`; it is the only part of a response body the server receives (besides captures).
   - `verifier.lookup`: a read operation that lists objects (for example refunds of a charge), its parameters as canonical action fields, and `correlate`, the JSON pointer of each item's field that equals the dispatch's idempotency key `pc-<transaction id>` (HR-008).
   - `verifier.expect`: pairs of an observed JSON pointer and a canonical action field (`params.amount.value`, `target.id`) that must be equal.
   - `verifier.states`: `{field, confirmed[], pending[], none[]}`.
   - `verifier.limits`: reviewed text stating what the verifier does not establish.
   - `dispatch.http.query`: query parameters built from canonical fields; values are encoded by the gateway, and the final URL check allows exactly the declared names.
   - `target_logs`: `[{operation, window_param, correlate, effect_of}]`, a reviewed read operation that lists objects created since a time, and which write operation they are effects of.
   - The mock-payments package gains `payments.refund.list` (refunds of a charge, or created since a time) and these fields; its golden tests and pinned digest change deliberately.
6. **Target-log reconciliation (HR-112).**
   - Every 15 minutes for each connection whose package declares a target log, the server creates a `target_log` verification task for the window since the last complete run, minus 5 minutes of overlap. The gateway pages through the listing (at most 1,000 items per run, reporting `complete`).
   - Each item is matched by its correlation value `pc-<transaction id>` to a transaction of that connection whose permit reached `DISPATCHING`. An item with no correlation value, or one naming no such transaction, is an **effect without a receipt**: an `unreceipted_effects` row, `security.effect_without_receipt` in the ledger, and a notification to org admins and security admins. A match to an `UNKNOWN` outcome is evidence for its reconciliation (it resolves `OCCURRED`); a match to a `failed` outcome makes that effect `CONFLICTING`.
   - A finding is not proof of an attack (a person may have used the target's own console), so it never quarantines anything by itself. M10 turns findings into alerts.
7. **Execution states (F471–F479).** Derived, not stored: `REQUESTED` (an open evaluation), `BLOCKED` (`DENY` or `CANNOT_AUTHORIZE`), `WAITING` (a hold), `AUTHORIZED` (a permit, not yet dispatching), `DISPATCHED` (from `DISPATCHING`, including an unknown outcome), `ACCEPTED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED` (a permit released unused, or a hold declined or expired: nothing was sent). The explorer always shows the execution state and the effect state side by side (F479).
8. **Checkpoints (HR-194, HR-111).**
   - A per-org job runs every `evidence.checkpoint_interval` when the org's chain has grown. In one transaction it locks the org's ledger head, recomputes the chain links of every entry since the last checkpoint from the entries themselves (a row edited before sealing is caught here), appends the new leaves to the tiles, computes the root, checks an RFC 9162 consistency proof from the previous checkpoint's tree to the new one, signs the note and stores it. Any mismatch stops checkpointing for that org, writes `security.evidence_integrity_failed` to the platform audit, notifies org and security admins, and shows the integrity status as failed until an operator investigates.
   - A daily job re-verifies each org's whole chain against its latest checkpoint (the M1 `ledger.Verify`, finally wired), and reports the same way.
   - Checkpoint keys are published in `evidence-keys.json` with their validity, so a re-signed history either uses a known key with a tree that conflicts with copies verifiers kept and with anchored roots, or an unknown key (T-073).
   - The Merkle code (tiles, inclusion and consistency proofs) is ours, on the standard library, with the RFC 9162 test vectors and known-answer tests against C2SP examples.
9. **Retention (HR-198, decision 4).**
   - Categories and defaults (minimum–maximum): `payloads` 7 days (1–30), `normalized_facts` (replay inputs and observed values) 90 days (7–730), `receipts` (receipt bodies and receipt ledger entries) 365 days (30–3,650), `approvals` 365 days (365–3,650), `security_audit` 365 days (365–3,650), `versions` (grant, policy, package and connection revisions: kept while any retained receipt references them; M7 never deletes them). Operational traces live outside the database (SB-4, 14 days).
   - A new login role `pc_retention` (bootstrap, like `pc_app`; its password from `database.retention_password_file`) has only `UPDATE (body columns, body_removed_at, removed_by_policy)` on the evidence tables and `DELETE` on `payload_captures` and `evaluation_inputs`, with RLS. Only the worker's retention job uses it. `pc_app` keeps no UPDATE or DELETE on evidence (HR-055).
   - The job runs daily per org through the audited lister. It never touches an item inside an active legal hold. It sets bodies to NULL, records when and under which policy revision, and writes one `evidence.retention_removed` ledger entry per batch (category, count, oldest and newest). Chain hashes, Merkle leaves and checkpoints stay, so `pclaw verify` still verifies the chain and reports "body removed by retention policy r on date" (F469, F521).
   - Lengthening retention applies at once. Shortening is a weakening change: a person holding `evidence.retention.manage`, audited, notified to org admins and auditors, and effective 7 days later, so a hold can still be placed. Creating a hold applies at once; releasing one is audited and notified.
   - Removed content is never rebuilt, and a request repeated after its evidence was removed is decided like any other new request (F520).
10. **Payload capture (HR-199, decision 7).**
    - A person holding `evidence.capture.manage` creates a capture profile: a purpose (required, at most 500 characters), the connections and operations it covers, request and/or response, a byte cap (at most 64 KiB per body), a retention (at most 30 days) and an expiry (at most 90 days). Creating and disabling are audited and notified to org admins and auditors. Profiles reach the gateways serving those connections in their configuration.
    - The gateway captures the outbound body it sent (built from the action; headers, including the credential, are never captured) and the target's response body after secret-echo redaction (HR-076), truncated to the cap, and sends them with `RecordExecution`. Monitor-mode and delegated outcomes capture nothing.
    - The server seals each capture with the org's `payload_captures` key (AES-256-GCM, AAD `org|payload_captures|content|id`, HR-062) and keeps it for the profile's retention.
    - `ReadPayloadCapture` needs `evidence.read_restricted` (human only), a reason, and writes `evidence.payload_read` (who, which capture, the reason) to the ledger before returning. Captures never appear in logs, job arguments, receipts, notifications or explorer responses, and in a pack only when its creator holds `evidence.read_restricted` and asks for them.
11. **Decision replay (HR-197, decision 5).**
    - The pipeline's `Reader` (every read of one evaluation) is wrapped in a recorder. The finalization stores what it recorded as one `evaluation_inputs` row in the same transaction as the receipt: the canonical action; the run, agent, grant chain, guardrails, connection, containment, claim, hold and variant reads; the facts and the counter and budget values read; and the policy and definitions by version and digest. It is sealed with the org's `evaluation_inputs` key (HR-062), capped at 32 KiB compressed (beyond that it is stored as `truncated` and replay is incomplete), and kept under `normalized_facts`.
    - `ReplayDecision` loads the inputs, rebuilds a reader that serves them, and runs steps 1–8 at the recorded time with the recorded policy, or with a given policy version (a proposed or later version, F505). It never calls the finalization, writes no transaction, receipt, reservation or permit, and never reaches a gateway (F508). It returns the replayed decision and checklist, whether they reproduce the original, and per check which inputs or policy rules differ (F506).
    - Incomplete replays say why (inputs removed by retention, truncated, written by an older pipeline version than the replaying code supports, or a policy version that no longer exists, F507).
    - Outcomes and checklists need `evidence.read`; the input values themselves only `evidence.read_restricted`. Replay runs at most 10 per minute per caller.
12. **Anchoring (HR-195, decision 3).**
    - Every `evidence.anchoring.interval`, for each org with a checkpoint, the job draws a 32-byte random nonce and computes the leaf `SHA-256(0x00 ‖ "pantherclaw.anchor-leaf.v1" ‖ nonce ‖ SHA-256(checkpoint note))`. The nonce changes every anchor, so a quiet org's leaf still changes, and the leaves show neither which org they belong to nor who was active. Leaves are ordered by their own value.
    - The anchor statement over the global root is signed by the `anchors` key (ECDSA P-256, platform scope, its own purpose) and sent to `rekor.url` as a `hashedrekord` v0.0.2 entry. The response's inclusion proof and checkpoint are checked against `rekor.public_key_file` and `rekor.origin` before the anchor counts as `ANCHORED`. The signature is timestamped by `tsa.url` (RFC 3161, SHA-256, with a nonce), and the token is checked against `tsa.cert_chain_file`.
    - Both clients are the M1 egress client (HR-070..072), to the configured hosts only, with timeouts. Failures retry with backoff, mark the anchor `FAILED`, log `evidence.anchor_failed` and appear in the integrity status. They never touch authorization.
    - Nothing is hard-coded: Sigstore will retire its 2025 Rekor v2 instance, so the operator configures the log and the timestamp authority. The defaults in the example configuration point at Sigstore's public services and say how to update them.
    - Our client is small and uses only the standard library: Rekor's JSON over HTTP, the RFC 9162 proofs from design decision 8, the C2SP signed-note checkpoint, and RFC 3161 with `encoding/asn1` and `crypto/x509`, limited to one signer, signed attributes and ECDSA or RSA. It gets fuzz tests and known-answer tests against recorded Sigstore responses.
    - Team edition: on a Community licence the setting is refused at start with a clear message.
13. **Offline verification (HR-196).**
    - `pclaw evidence trust --out trust.json` fetches `evidence-keys.json` from the deployment once, prints every key's fingerprint for comparison through another channel, and writes the trust file. `pclaw verify` never fetches anything: it uses that trust file and, for anchors, a Sigstore `trusted_root.json` the user supplies (`--sigstore-trusted-root`).
    - `pclaw evidence bundle` (`ExportBundle`) writes, for chosen transactions or a sequence range: the receipts, the ledger entries with their links, inclusion proofs to a checkpoint, consistency proofs between the checkpoints included, and, when anchored, the org's leaf, nonce, the anchor's leaves, Rekor entry and timestamp token. A user may add a checkpoint they saved earlier (`--previous`), which `pclaw verify` checks for consistency (a witness).
    - The report lists each check: receipt signatures and key validity, chain links, inclusion, consistency, checkpoint signatures (and the ML-DSA line when present and trusted), the anchor leaf and global inclusion, Rekor's checkpoint and inclusion, the timestamp. Each check is `passed`, `failed` or `not available` (for example a body removed by retention, or not anchored yet), and the exit code is non-zero if any check failed.
    - The report ends with fixed limits text (F510, F511): integrity of what the bundle contains, issued by the holder of these keys; not completeness, not that an external effect happened, not certification.
14. **HR-110 wording.** HR-110 and ARCHITECTURE §12 say the chainer takes an advisory lock. Since M1 it locks the org's `ledger_heads` row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) and River runs one chain job per org, which gives the same single writer per org. This PR changes the wording to "a per-org lock on the ledger head"; the guarantee is unchanged.
15. **Evidence packs (HR-196, F525–F532, Team).**
    - `CreateEvidencePack` (human only, `evidence.export`) takes a scope (transactions, a run, an agent, a time range) and what to include (receipts, versions, approvals, containment and restoration, captures). A job builds it from the creator's permissions at that moment, so the pack contains only what they could read, and the manifest says which filters applied (F515).
    - The manifest lists each item with its SHA-256; the effect states of every transaction, including unknown, conflicting and pending (F528); the grant, policy, definition, approval and connection revisions involved (F527); containment and restoration events (F529); the exporter, time, retention limits and redactions (F530); every gap with its reason (removed, outside permission, not captured, not anchored, F532); the latest checkpoint and anchor with proofs; and the fixed sentence "This pack supports review; it does not certify compliance."
    - The manifest is a JWS signed by the `evidence_packs` key (and co-signed with ML-DSA-65 when enabled). Content is a ZIP of canonical JSON files, at most 64 MiB, downloadable for 7 days. Creation and every download are audited.
    - Graphs (F531) are exported only as the edges the receipts pin (grant lineage, transaction links).
16. **ML-DSA co-signatures (decision 6, PN-007.5).** When `evidence.mldsa_cosign` is on (Enterprise), checkpoints and pack manifests also carry an ML-DSA-65 signature from a `checkpoints_pq` key (`crypto/mldsa`, Go 1.27). Receipts do not: they keep `sigs[]` reserved, and they are covered by the checkpoints through the Merkle tree. The FEATURES row becomes MVP for checkpoints and manifests.
17. **Keys.** New purposes: `anchors` (ECDSA P-256), `evidence_packs` (Ed25519), `checkpoints_pq` (ML-DSA-65); all platform scope, wrapped by the `KeyProvider`, rotated like the others (90 days, 7-day overlap, SB-3) and published in `evidence-keys.json` with their validity. The existing `checkpoints` key is used for the first time.
18. **Authorization and tenancy.** Each use case checks its permission. Explorer, reconciliation and replay calls check the scope like `run.read`. Ids from another org return NotFound (T-037). `ClaimVerifications` and `ReportObservation` take the org and gateway from the certificate only, and a report for a lease of another gateway is refused. `anchors` is the one global table; it holds only blinded leaves and the global root. Every new tenant table gets the cross-tenant tests.
19. **Logging and evidence.** Audited (ledger): reconciliation resolutions and releases, transaction links, unreceipted-effect findings, checkpoint integrity failures, anchor failures, retention changes and removals, hold creation and release, capture profile creation and disabling, payload reads, pack creation and downloads. Observed field values, captures, replay inputs, bases written by people, Rekor and timestamp responses are never logged; logs carry ids and digests.
20. **Failure semantics.** Verification unavailable: the effect stays at the level reached, tasks wait, and nothing is resolved by default. Checkpointing fails: receipts are still written and chained (authorization never waits for proof), and the integrity status says checkpointing is behind or failed. Anchoring fails: `not anchored` in every report. Replay inputs missing: an incomplete replay. Retention role unavailable: nothing is removed, and the job reports it.
21. **Stacking on M5 part 2.** Slice A11 (the release page and waitlist linkage) is stacked on M5 part 2 slices 208 (binding ceremony) and 210 (waitlist producers). Everything else in track A, and all of track B, depends only on `main`.
22. **Performance.** The authorization path gains one sealed insert per evaluation (replay inputs) and one insert per execution (the receipt body). Everything else (verification, checkpoints, anchors, retention, packs) runs in the worker. The exit re-measures `Authorize` and records the cost in `docs/perf/M7.md`.

**Dependencies to add:** none. Merkle trees, signed notes, ECDSA, ML-DSA (`crypto/mldsa`), ASN.1, X.509 and ZIP come from the standard library (decision 3). DEPENDENCY_POLICY §3 is unchanged.

**Founder decisions (asked 2026-10-10 before the brief was written; the recommended option each time).** Each one says which code it blocks (GATES_AND_REVIEW §5).
1. **Who makes follow-up reads of the target.**
   - **(a) Recommended:** the gateway that serves the connection, with the connection's sealed credential, only the package's reviewed read operation, on a single-use task the server leases to it over mTLS; it reports only the declared fields; never a write.
   - **(b)** The agent or SDK reports what it observed (untrusted; labelled unverified).
   - **(c)** No automatic verification in M7; people resolve every outcome.

   **Answer: (a).** *Blocks:* A7, A8.
2. **How an `UNKNOWN` outcome is resolved.**
   - **(a) Recommended:** evidence that the effect happened resolves it automatically (commit). "It did not happen" frees the budget only when a person who is not the run's launcher, represented user or agent owner confirms on a page with a security-key step-up and a written basis.
   - **(b)** Fully automatic in both directions from verifier evidence.
