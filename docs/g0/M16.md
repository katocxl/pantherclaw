### G0 — M16 — Data-flow holds, external policy checks and evidence-backed suggestions — 2026-10-29

This brief is written before implementation (G0). It lists planned scope; delivery status is tracked by the M16 pull requests and, at the end of the milestone, in the exit status section. It builds on M15 ([G0 M15](M15.md)): the design was written on `main` at 5225b371, and M16 lands after M15, so its migrations start at 00196. The founder answered four open decisions for M16 (F5–F8) on 2026-10-29, the recommended option each time; they are recorded at the end as answered. Every other choice is a design decision made by the coordinator (D70–D92 in the coordinator's tracker), summarised below.

**Purpose:** PantherClaw decides each action on its own. It can limit where an agent sends data (destination classes, disclosure limits) and what it may read (M14's data scoping), but it does not connect the two: an agent that reads customer pii and then posts to a public destination is caught only by an alert-only detection after the fact. Customers also cannot bring their own judgement into a decision: their data classifications, their existing Open Policy Agent policies and the identity-risk signals they already receive stay outside the decision path. And posture findings say that a grant holds authority it never uses, but leave the person to work out what to change and what that change would break. M16 adds a read history per run tree and holds an external disclosure after a sensitive read unless the grant allows that data flow; it lets classification facts, an external OPA check and identity-risk signals restrict a decision, and never widen it; and it turns unused-authority findings into suggestions that carry their evidence, coverage limits, expected effect and uncertainty, and that can only become a policy draft. In product terms this is Guardrails ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)).

**Scope (planned, D70):**
- **16a Read history and the data-flow hold** (F123). Every allowed read records the class of data it returned; an external disclosure after a `pii` or `secret` read anywhere in the same run tree is held for approval unless a flow the grant allows covers it. Monitor mode records the would-hold.
- **16b Outside signals** (F366, F7, F8). Classification facts with provenance that can only raise a read's class; an external Open Policy Agent check, synchronous, restrict-only and fail closed; identity-risk facts from the Shared Signals receiver that policy can use to restrict.
- **16c Evidence-backed suggestions** (F185). Unused-authority findings become suggestions with an evidence window, coverage limits, observed operations, expected effect, affected owners and uncertainty; a person can dismiss one or turn it into a policy draft, which goes through the M11.1 publication gate.

**What "confidential or higher" means (D71).** The founder chose "package-declared data classes at or above confidential" as the meaning of a sensitive read (F5). PantherClaw's data classes, from M14, are ordered `public < internal < pii < secret`; there is no class called confidential. The first class above `internal` is `pii`, so **a sensitive read is a read whose class is `pii` or `secret`**.

Editions: the external policy check and suggestions are Business (D81, D92); M16 adds no other edition gate.

**Out of scope:**
- sliding time windows for the data-flow hold (the window is the rest of the run tree, F6);
- external checks that can allow or widen anything, and checks that fail open (F7);
- risk signals that allow anything (F8);
- suggestion kinds other than unused authority, and model-drafted suggestions (M21);
- automatic publication of any suggestion;
- the web console screens for flows, checks and suggestions (private console stream).

*The data-flow hold* (TB1; D72–D76).
- **Information disclosure (T-190):** an agent reads pii in one run and discloses it from a child run, a sibling run or a later step, hoping the reads are counted per run. Answered by: reads count across the whole run tree that shares the run's root (HR-431).
- **Elevation (T-197):** a child grant claims a wider flow allowance than its parent. Answered by: flows only narrow under delegation, and none means none (HR-432).
- **Repudiation:** read history stores classes and transaction ids; the hold names the classes and read transactions, never content (HR-434).

*The external policy check* (TB8; D81).
- **Tampering and elevation (T-192):** a malicious or compromised OPA endpoint answers `allow` to widen a decision, or answers with text meant for the agent. Answered by: the check can only restrict, its `allow` is a passed checklist item and nothing more (HR-436); reasons are untrusted and shown to people only (HR-440); the endpoint is allow-listed by server configuration, called through the egress guards and active only after a step-up (HR-438).
- **Denial of service (T-193):** an outage or a slow endpoint, hoping decisions pass while it is down. Answered by: a timeout, error, bad answer or too many calls in flight is CANNOT_AUTHORIZE (HR-437); at most three active checks per organization and a 1-second timeout ceiling bound the latency added.
- **Information disclosure (T-194):** prompt or message content sent to a third-party policy service. Answered by: disclosure-capable parameters are replaced by their length (HR-439).

*Outside facts* (TB8; D80, D82).
- **Tampering (T-191):** a classification fact that marks a sensitive target as public to avoid the hold. Answered by: classification facts need provenance and can only raise a class (HR-435).
- **Spoofing (T-195):** a forged identity-risk fact used to lock a user out, or to lift a restriction. Answered by: identity-risk facts come only from the signals receiver's built-in provider, which no RPC can create, and can only restrict (HR-441, HR-261).

*Suggestions* (TB4; D90–D92).
- **Elevation (T-196):** a suggestion, or a person acting on one, loosens policy, or a suggestion claims certainty it does not have. Answered by: a suggestion never changes policy and drafting creates a draft that must pass the M11.1 gate (HR-442); every suggestion states its window, coverage limits and uncertainty (HR-443); expected effect comes only from recorded transactions (HR-445).
- **Information disclosure:** suggestions are visible only to people who can see the grant and the agent (HR-444).

**Hardening rules in scope:** the new rules **HR-430..HR-445** added to [HARDENING_RULES](../security/HARDENING_RULES.md) by this brief, plus HR-045..047 (the grant lattice), HR-070..077 (egress), HR-079 (destination classes and disclosure), HR-160 (facts), HR-206 (unknown facts), HR-230 and HR-231 (the publication gate), HR-255 (posture findings), HR-259 (step-up activation of outbound destinations), HR-261 (the Shared Signals receiver), HR-302 (data scope obligations), HR-400 (agent-safe refusals) and HR-004 and HR-050..057 for the new tables.

**Design decisions:**

*S16a — read history and the data-flow hold*

1. **D72: the read class** (HR-430, HR-434). On every ALLOW or ALLOW_WITH_OBLIGATIONS decision of an `access: read` operation, the decision records a `read_class`:
   - the strictest class among the definition's declared `Response` fields, capped by the decision's `data_classes` obligation when there is one;
   - for an operation that declares `Scoping` and has a `data_classes` obligation, the strictest class the obligation allows;
   - raised, never lowered, by a `pc.classification` fact about the action's target (D80);
   - otherwise empty (NULL).

   Migration 00196 adds `pc.transactions.read_class` (NULL or one of `public`, `internal`, `pii`, `secret`) and a partial index on `(org_id, run_id)` where the class is `pii` or `secret`. The content of a read is never recorded.
2. **D73: the run tree** (HR-431). Sensitive reads count across the whole run tree: every run that shares the current run's root (the root from the run's ancestors, descendants by a recursive query over the run children, depth at most 8). A new pipeline reader method `SensitiveReads(org, run)` returns the 20 most recent sensitive reads of the tree, each with its transaction id, run id, operation, class and time. Every reader implements it: the Postgres reader, the recorder and recorded reader, replay, the policy scenario fixture and the pipeline test helpers.
3. **D74: external disclosure.** An action is an external disclosure when its operation is `access: write` and either its connection's destination class is `public` (HR-079) or its predicted consequences include an `external_disclosure` effect. Reads never count.
4. **D75: allowed flows** (HR-432). A grant gains `Flows`, a list of flow allowances, each with `Classes` (a subset of `pii` and `secret`) and `Destinations` (at least one exact destination, kind and id). Flows are not part of a grant's bounds and are not on envelopes.
   - No flows means no flow is allowed.
   - Under delegation each child allowance must be covered by one parent allowance (classes and destinations both subsets); otherwise issuance fails with the existing lattice error.
   - An action passes when one allowance covers every sensitive class read in the run tree and every ActionIR destination of the action; an action without destinations never matches.
   - Flows are stored with the grant revision, as its other limits are (migration 00197 only if those are columns); `grants.proto` gains `repeated FlowAllowance flows` on the grant and on its issue request.
5. **D76: the hold** (HR-431, HR-433). A new pipeline step in `internal/authority/pipeline/dataflow.go`, called in the boundaries stage right after the disclosure step: an external disclosure (D74) with sensitive reads in the run tree (D73) and no covering flow (D75) adds the requirement REQUIRE_APPROVAL with the code `DATA_FLOW_HOLD` and level `data_flow`. The detail names the classes and the read transaction ids, never content. An approval binds the action as usual.
   - Monitor mode records a would-hold with the code `DATA_FLOW_HOLD`, by generalising the pipeline's existing would-unknown result into a general would-result. If the receipt's would field cannot carry it without a receipt-format change, the builder stops and asks the coordinator.
   - The existing alert-only read-then-disclose detection stays.

*S16b — classification facts, the external OPA check and identity-risk facts*

6. **D80: classification facts** (HR-435).
   - A fact declaration gains a `kind`: `value` (the default) or `classification`, whose type is the data-classes enum.
   - Facts gain provenance: `source` (at most 128 characters) and `method` (`manual`, `rule` or `model`), added by migration 00198. A classification fact without both is refused when it is accepted.
   - The pipeline reads `pc.classification` about the action's target with the other current facts, as optional: a missing fact has no effect, and a stale one is ignored with a checklist note.
   - A classification fact raises the read class (D72, through S16a's function) and is visible to policy as `facts["pc.classification"]`. It never lowers a class and never allows anything.
7. **D81: the external policy check** (founder decision F7; HR-436..440).
   - **Records.** Migration 00199 `pc.external_policy_checks`: `org_id, id, name, endpoint, ca_pem (optional), timeout_ms (50–1000, default 250), operations (one or more glob patterns), state (PENDING, ACTIVE or DISABLED), created_by, activated_by, created_at, updated_at`. At most three checks are ACTIVE per organization. The endpoint must be `https` and start with a prefix from the server configuration `external_checks.allowed_prefixes`.
   - **API.** `ExternalCheckService` (`external_checks.proto`): `CreateExternalCheck`, `ActivateExternalCheck` (with a security-key step-up, like SIEM destinations), `DisableExternalCheck`, `ListExternalChecks` and `TestExternalCheck` (with a synthetic input). Business edition (`ExternalPolicyChecks` in the Business entitlements); create and activate refuse below it.
   - **In the pipeline.** A new port `ExternalCheck`, modelled on the peer-delegation check, called in the boundaries stage after the policy rules for actions whose operation matches an ACTIVE check (`internal/authority/pipeline/external.go`).
   - **Input.** A POST of `{"input": {...}}` with `version "pc.opa.v1"`, the organization, operation, access, target (type and id), destinations, destination class, principal (agent, instance, run, represented user), mode and the material parameters, in which every disclosure-capable parameter is replaced by `{"bytes": n}`.
   - **Output.** `{"result": {"decision": "allow" | "deny" | "hold", "reasons": [...]}}` with at most 5 reasons of at most 200 characters. `deny` is DENY `EXTERNAL_POLICY_DENY`; `hold` is REQUIRE_APPROVAL `EXTERNAL_POLICY_HOLD`; `allow` only passes a checklist item. A timeout, a non-2xx answer, bad JSON or a missing result is CANNOT_AUTHORIZE `EXTERNAL_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`.
   - Reasons are UNTRUSTED: shown to people only, never to agents (HR-400).
   - **Client.** The egress client with the check's CA and timeout; at most 16 calls in flight per organization, beyond which the decision is CANNOT_AUTHORIZE.
   - Replay and simulation never call the check; they report "external check not evaluated".
8. **D82: identity-risk facts** (founder decision F8; HR-441).
   - When the Shared Signals receiver accepts a signal, it also writes the fact `pc.identity.risk` about `user:<id>`: `{event, level: "high", at}`.
   - The fact comes from a built-in provider kind `builtin` (name `pc.signals`, one per organization, created on first use); fact validation allows this third provider form, and no RPC can create it.
   - The pipeline reads it about the run's represented user and about the launching user when that is a person, and exposes it to policy as `principal.risk {open, event, at}`. A fact older than 24 hours counts as absent. It never allows anything.
   - A sample rule is given below; no template ships one.

*S16c — evidence-backed suggestions*

9. **D90: the suggestions table** (migration 00200 `pc.policy_suggestions`): `org_id, id, kind (only UNUSED_AUTHORITY), posture_finding_id, grant_id, agent_id, owners, window_from, window_to, observed (operation → count, at most 100), coverage_limits (routes not ENFORCED in the window, at most 100), proposed (the restriction draft's rules), expected_effect ({matching_actions, newly_denied, newly_held} from recorded transactions in the window), uncertainty (LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH), uncertainty_reasons, state (OPEN, DRAFTED, DISMISSED or STALE), draft_policy_version_id, dismissed_by, dismiss_reason, created_at, updated_at`. At most one open suggestion per posture finding.
10. **D91: generation** (HR-443, HR-445).
    - When posture records an unused-authority finding, a River job enqueued in the same transaction creates or updates its suggestion, over a 30-day window.
    - Uncertainty is LOW only when every route of the grant's operations was ENFORCED or OBSERVE_ONLY for the whole window; MEDIUM when any was PARTIAL; HIGH when any was UNKNOWN or the window has gaps.
    - A resolved finding makes its suggestion STALE.
    - The expected effect counts only recorded transactions; data that was not collected is reported as "not collected", never as zero.
11. **D92: the API** (HR-442, HR-444).
    - `SuggestionService` (`suggestions.proto`): `ListSuggestions` (state and kind filters, keyset paging), `GetSuggestion`, `DismissSuggestion` (with a reason) and `DraftFromSuggestion`.
    - `DraftFromSuggestion` creates a policy draft with the existing restriction-draft code of the security steps and never publishes it: the M11.1 publication gate applies. The suggestion becomes DRAFTED with the draft's id.
    - Permissions as above; Business edition through the posture-remediation entitlement.
    - CLI: `pclaw suggestions list|show|dismiss|draft`.

**Sample policy (D82).** A rule, in the policy JSON format the templates use, that holds payments and outside posts while the run's represented or launching person has an open identity-risk signal:

```json
{
  "id": "hold-on-identity-risk", "per": "org", "kind": "REQUIRE_APPROVAL",
  "summary": "an independent approver approves payments and outside posts while the person behind the run has an open identity-risk signal",
  "operations": ["stripe.refund.create", "stripe.transfer.create", "slack.message.post"],
  "when": "principal.risk.open", "reason": "IDENTITY_RISK",
  "approval": {"role": "approver", "count": 1, "independent": true}
}
```
