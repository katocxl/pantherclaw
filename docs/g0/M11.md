**Purpose:** today a policy version goes from draft straight to published, and the published version decides every agent's actions at once. Nothing checks that a version was tested, nothing shows what it will change, and one person can loosen it. M11.1 makes policy changes staged and reviewable. A version advances draft → validated → tested → published, and the publication gate re-runs validation and the version's own scenario tests (with mutation testing, HR-044) in the publish call. A classifier compares the new version with the published one: a tightening is published by one publisher; anything that is not provably tighter needs confirmations with security keys from two people when the organization has two publishers. Before a version is enforced everywhere, a simulation replays it over recorded decisions, a shadow rollout replays live traffic without enforcing anything, and a pilot enforces it for named cohorts of agents first, with a status per agent. A rollback keeps emergency rules, and temporary exceptions waive one marked rule for one agent, at most for 30 days. Three task templates ship with tests. In product terms this is Guardrails' policy lifecycle ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)).

**Scope (planned):**

- **Bundle additions** (S1a, `internal/policy/domain`). `Rule.Exceptable bool` (`json:"exceptable,omitzero"`), `Rule.Emergency bool` (`json:"emergency,omitzero"`; refused on ANNOTATE rules, design decision 4) and `Bundle.Tests []TestCase` (`json:"tests,omitzero"`). `ContentDigest(b)` is the SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 form ([ADR-0011](../adr/0011-strict-json-canonicalization.md)) of `{"id","rules","tests"}` (the version is excluded); `RuleDigest(r)` is the SHA-256 of the rule's canonical form. - **The classifier** (S1a, `internal/policy/domain/diff.go`). `Compare(base, next *Bundle) Diff` (base nil means no policy: every rule is ADDED) returns one `Change{RuleID, Kind ADDED|REMOVED|CHANGED, Field, Before, After, Direction, Sentence}` per change, with a direction TIGHTER, LOOSER, UNKNOWN or NEUTRAL, matched by rule id:
  - adding a rule that is not ANNOTATE is TIGHTER and removing one is LOOSER; ANNOTATE rules, `summary` and `reason` text, tests and labels are NEUTRAL;
  - operations and environments: a superset is TIGHTER, a subset LOOSER, anything else UNKNOWN (empty means all, the widest); any textual change of `when` is UNKNOWN;
  - kind (design decision 23): only a change to FORBID is TIGHTER (FORBID denies every action it matches, so the requirement or constraint fields it drops do not count as changes); FORBID → any other kind is LOOSER; every other kind change (CONSTRAIN ↔ REQUIRE_APPROVAL or REQUIRE_STEP_UP, REQUIRE_APPROVAL ↔ REQUIRE_STEP_UP) is UNKNOWN, because a CONSTRAIN violation is a DENY while a REQUIRE_* match is a hold an approver can release;
  - constraints: a lower `amount_max` or `count_max` (same parameter and currency) is TIGHTER and a higher one LOOSER; a smaller allowed, target or destination set is TIGHTER and a larger one LOOSER; a change of parameter, currency or constraint kind, or any other set change, is UNKNOWN;
  - a higher verify level, more approvals, independence or a change window switched on, a shorter deadline, facts added or a lower `max_age` are TIGHTER, and the reverse LOOSER; a change of approval role or of step-up subject or method is UNKNOWN;
  - `exceptable` false → true is LOOSER and true → false TIGHTER; `emergency` true → false is LOOSER and false → true TIGHTER.

  `Diff.Direction` is TIGHTER when every change is TIGHTER or NEUTRAL and at least one is TIGHTER; NEUTRAL when all are NEUTRAL; otherwise LOOSER when any change is LOOSER, else UNKNOWN. `Loosens()` is true for LOOSER and UNKNOWN. Sentences are plain, for example "Rule hold-large-refunds: approval threshold 50.00 USD → 100.00 USD (looser)".

**Security constraints and design decisions:**
1. **States** (S1b, HR-230, F184). DRAFT → VALIDATED (validation passed with the current digests) → TESTED (tests passed) → PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED, each a conditional update (HR-004). VALIDATED and TESTED never go back; a stale result makes the publish call re-run them. The store always sets the content digest on insert, so a version from before 00110 can never be published again; an organization's currently published pre-00110 version stays published until a new one supersedes it.
2. **Bundle additions** (S1a). `exceptable` and `emergency` per rule, `tests` per bundle, the content and rule digests, as in "Contracts".
3. **The classifier** (S1a). As in "Contracts", with an exhaustive table test. A change it cannot prove tighter counts as loosening.
4. **Emergency carry-forward** (S1a, S1b; HR-236). A rule with the field `emergency: true` is an emergency rule; validation refuses the field on ANNOTATE rules, and labels stay ANNOTATE-only as today. `CarryEmergency(current, target)` returns the target plus every emergency rule of the current version (replacing a rule of the same id) and the carried ids. A rollback always carries them. Publishing a version that drops or changes an emergency rule of the published version is LOOSER and needs the publisher to hold `incident.respond` as well as the confirmations of decision 11.
5. **Decision categories** (S1a, `internal/authority/replay/classify.go`). Severity ALLOW 0 < ALLOW_WITH_OBLIGATIONS 1 < REQUIRE_STEP_UP 2 < REQUIRE_APPROVAL 3 < DENY 4 = CANNOT_AUTHORIZE 4. `Classify(before, after, beforeReason, afterReason)`: same decision and reason → UNCHANGED; higher severity → NEWLY_DENIED, NEWLY_HELD or NEWLY_CONSTRAINED by the new decision; lower severity → NEWLY_ALLOWED (expanded exposure, F189); same severity with another decision or reason → REASON_CHANGED. Callers mark incomplete replays UNEVALUABLE. Used by shadow and simulation.
6. **The publication gate** (S1b; HR-230, F1). `PublishPolicyVersion` (human, `policy.publish`) loads the version; re-runs validation and tests with the current definitions and catalog in the call and records both rows; refuses with `POLICY_VALIDATION_FAILED` or `POLICY_TESTS_FAILED` (findings and failing cases in the error details), leaving the state unchanged; advances the state as each passes; compares with the published version. A version that does not loosen is published in one transaction with the audit event, by an update that requires state TESTED, the same content digest and a passing test run of that digest. A loosening creates a PUBLISH change request with the published version as its base, returns it, and leaves the version TESTED. Without a validator or test runner the publish is refused (fail closed). The same gate runs on every path that makes a version enforced: pilot and completion (S2), rollback, template apply (S5) and the development seed. `ValidatePolicyVersion` and `TestPolicyVersion` advance the state when they pass; `DiffPolicyVersions` returns the classified changes and the sample outcome diff. A hook `Versions.OnPublished(ctx, tx, org, version, base)` for S2 runs inside the publishing transaction, both on a direct publish and when a PUBLISH change request is applied (nil is a no-op).
7. **Scenario tests** (S1a; HR-044, HR-232, HR-238). The runner evaluates each case with the real `pipeline.EvaluateWith` over an in-memory fixture reader: containment clear; a VERIFIED agent at attestation L2 with an active run; one root grant covering the operation, with the case's constraints; usage from `given.budget`; facts from `given.facts`; the organization's current active definition for the operation, read once and read-only; the version under test compiled with the tenant's CEL budget; a connection in enforce mode; holds from `given.approval`. A case passes when the decision and the decisive reason (and the rule, when given) match. The gate passes only when every case passes, every HR-044 mutant (each comparison in each `when`) is killed by at least one case, and every rule that is not ANNOTATE has a removal mutant that is killed. Bounds: 200 cases and 20 000 evaluations per run, mutants included; beyond them the run fails with `TESTS_TOO_LARGE`. The runner reads only definitions and the catalog, never finalizes and never writes.
8. **Validation and sample outcomes** (S1a, `internal/policy/validate`; F196, F197, HR-238, R-25). Errors (block publication): compile errors, `OPERATION_UNKNOWN`, `CONSTRAINT_UNSUPPORTED` (a constraint a matched definition does not declare), `VERIFY_UNREACHABLE`, `FACT_UNKNOWN`, `CONFLICT` (two CONSTRAIN rules on the same parameter whose allowed, target or destination sets do not intersect, or `amount_max` in different currencies, for an action both match, with the example). Warnings: `SHADOWED`, `APPROVER_NONE`, `FACT_NO_SOURCE`. Info: `REDUNDANT`. Samples per operation come from the definition's parameter schema with boundary values around every numeric or money literal and constraint maximum, each allowed value plus one outside, and equality string literals, with facts present with literal values: at most 256 per operation and 4 096 per run. Every sample-based finding says so. `OutcomeDiff(base, next)` reports the changed outcomes over the same samples, with the categories of decision 5, using the policy engine only.
9. **Rollback** (S1b; F206, F207, HR-236). `RollbackPolicy(to_version_id)` (human, `policy.publish`) creates a draft with the target's rules and tests plus the carried emergency rules, then follows decision 6: a looser result becomes a PUBLISH change request, a tighter one is published. The response lists the carried rule ids. Containment, grants and envelopes are separate records and are never touched; the CLI says so.
10. **Callers of the old publish** (S1b). Every product path (the policies RPC, `pclaw policy publish`, template apply and remove, the development seed in `internal/server/devholds.go`) goes through the gate; the seed's bundle gets baseline tests that pass it. Test setup that needs only "some policy published" uses the test-only helper `internal/policy/policytest.Publish`, which inserts the version and a passing test run for its digest and publishes through the store; the gate's own tests use the real path.
11. **Change requests** (S1b; HR-231, F1, F2). One mechanism for PUBLISH, ROLLOUT and EXCEPTION. Required confirmations: 2 when the organization has at least two active persons holding `policy.publish` at the organization path when the request is made, else 1. Only persons holding `policy.publish` confirm, each with a security-key BINDING ceremony over the request's binding; persons and credentials must be distinct (`SAME_PERSON`, `SAME_KEY`, as for the kill switch). The requester's confirmation counts when they hold `policy.publish`. On the last confirmation, in one transaction: the base still holds (for PUBLISH and ROLLOUT the published version is still the base, else VOIDED `BASE_CHANGED`; the definitions and catalog digests are still those of the recorded test run, else VOIDED `STALE_TESTS`; for EXCEPTION the rule still exists in the published version, is `exceptable` and has the same rule digest, and the agent is not retired, else VOIDED `BASE_CHANGED`, and the requested expiry has not passed, else EXPIRED), the kind's apply callback runs, the request becomes APPROVED and `policy.change_approved` is audited. Any eligible confirmer may decline with a reason. Requests expire 24 hours after creation, checked by the database clock at use. Every RPC that creates a request returns its URL.
12. **Rollouts, cohorts and per-agent selection** (S2; F200–F205, F3, HR-234; shadow, pilots and cohorts are Team). Rollout flows are human and need `policy.publish`. `StartRollout` enters SHADOW (no cohorts means every agent). `StartPilot` runs the gate and the classifier; a looser version becomes a ROLLOUT change request whose apply enters PILOT, otherwise PILOT starts at once. `ExpandPilot` needs no new approval while the base is unchanged. `ExcludeTarget` marks an agent EXCEPTION, which stays on the published version. `CompleteRollout` publishes through the gate (the base must still be published, else `BASE_CHANGED`). `CancelRollout` returns the cohort agents to the published version without approval. In Community every publish creates a COMPLETE rollout whose targets are every agent that is not retired, for status only. Selection happens in pgauthority: an agent in an ENFORCE cohort of the organization's PILOT rollout whose target is not EXCEPTION gets the rollout version, everyone else the published version; when the rollout version cannot be compiled for an evaluation, the published version decides (FALLBACK). Nothing about selection comes from the request. Target states are set by a River sweeper (every minute, per due organization, at most 1 000 evaluations per tick): ACKNOWLEDGED when a decision for the agent was made under the rollout version (from the recording's policy reference or the receipt basis; receipts do not change); FAILED when a cohort agent was decided with FALLBACK (the owner is set and notified, `policy.rollout_target_failed`); VERIFIED when ACKNOWLEDGED and the agent's latest unexpired coverage snapshots for the version's rule operations are all PARTIAL or ENFORCED (the weakest is stored; no snapshot keeps the target ACKNOWLEDGED with reason `COVERAGE_UNKNOWN`). The authority never writes these rows.
13. **Shadow** (S2; F200, F203, HR-232, HR-233, HR-237, R-26). For a SHADOW rollout, or the SHADOW-mode cohorts of a pilot, the sweeper replays each new evaluation of an in-scope agent with the version and once with the recorded policy, classifies the pair with decision 5 (not reproduced or incomplete: UNEVALUABLE with the limitation codes) and writes a shadow result: at most 500 replays per tick and 100 000 rows per rollout, then daily counters only. Nothing changes on the hot path: a test proves the decision, basis digest and checklist are identical with and without an active shadow. `GetShadowSummary` gives counts by category, operation, agent and day, newly held per day (approval burden) and samples with ids, decisions and reasons only, never input values; every response and CLI line says "shadow prediction, not enforcement".
14. **Exceptions** (S3; F208–F210, HR-235, F2). A person holding `policy.exception.request` asks for an exception to a rule that exists and is `exceptable` in the published version, for one agent and 1–16 exact operations inside the rule's scope, with a reason, an owner who is a person and a requested expiry at most 30 days ahead; the request creates an EXCEPTION change request whose apply activates it. `expires_at` is the earlier of the requested expiry and activation plus 30 days, set by the database clock. `policy.publish` may revoke at any time; there is no renewal or extension, only a new request. The pipeline's policy read (after selection) loads the agent's ACTIVE, unexpired exceptions in the snapshot transaction and waives a rule only when it is `exceptable` in the selected version, its rule digest matches and the operation is listed. The engine still requires the rule's facts (missing or stale facts are not evaluated, as today, and never waived). A waived rule whose condition matches is reported WAIVED with `EXCEPTION_APPLIED` and the exception id, has no effect, and its obligations are dropped. Grants, envelopes, containment, identity, budgets, coverage and route mode are untouched, with a test for each. Replay serves the recorded exceptions. Built in two phases: new files from `origin/main` first, then the hook into selection and the engine after S1b and S2 push.
15. **Simulation** (S4; F187–F192, F287, HR-232, HR-237; Team). A simulation covers a window of at most 30 days inside retention and at most 50 000 evaluations (10 000 by default), one QUEUED or RUNNING per organization. The job takes decision receipts in the window, newest first, up to the cap, replays each with the recorded policy and with the version (as in decision 13), and classifies them; results are stored as counts, by workflow (per operation and per agent), the approval burden (newly held per day and by required role) and at most 25 samples per category and operation. Reports keep newly denied, held, constrained and allowed (flagged "expanded exposure"), unevaluable and changed reasons apart, and state the limits (F192): "predicts decisions, not agent replanning or business outcomes". Outputs carry no recorded input values; drill-down goes through the existing `ReplayDecision` with its own permission. Built in two phases: a local copy of decision 5's signature until S1a pushes, then S1a merged and the stand-in deleted.

### G0 — M11 part 2 — Governed automations, rest of M11, universal search — 2026-10-15

**Purpose:** today every grant comes from a person, and nothing in PantherClaw starts work on its own. Customers who want an agent launched for each support ticket, a grant revoked when a task closes, an approval chased before it expires or a digest delivered every Monday script it outside PantherClaw, with a long-lived credential and no record of why the work started. M11 part 2 adds governed automations. An automation is a versioned definition (When, If, Under authority, Do, Wait when, If work fails, Finish when) with its own execution identity. It is enabled only through a security-key ceremony by people who hold every permission its steps need; each execution re-checks that authority and, when it launches an agent, gets a fresh, short-lived grant inside an issuance ceiling those people approved. Triggers are schedules, platform events, conditions, thresholds, manual starts and signed inbound events from sources an admin added. Steps come from a closed catalogue; tool calls happen only through enrolled agents and the gateway, never from the server. Executions are tested, simulated, shadowed and rolled out to a cohort before everyone, and finish SUCCEEDED only with evidence. The rest of M11 lands with it: fleet cohorts, the production promotion gate, protected changes beyond policies, the compromised-publisher response and two reference packs. Universal search gains the remaining entities, quick find and rule-based plain-language questions. In product terms this is Guardrails' automations and the rest of Reflex's search ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)).

*The inbound event endpoint* (`POST /v1/automations/events/{org}/{source}`, TB8, D21). It is the second unauthenticated-by-session inbound endpoint, after M10's Shared Signals receiver, and the first whose requests start work.
- **Who can reach it:** anyone on the network. The only credential is the per-source signature.
- **Spoofing:** a request for an unknown, PENDING or DISABLED source; a forged or missing signature; a signature made with another source's secret. Answered by: only ACTIVE sources that a person activated with a step-up; a Standard Webhooks HMAC-SHA256 signature checked in constant time against that source's current secret (or the previous one during a rotation overlap of at most 24 hours); an unknown or inactive source gets a uniform 404 (HR-275, T-122).
- **Tampering and replay:** a captured request sent again, later, or with a changed body. Answered by: a timestamp within ±5 minutes; a `webhook-id` unique per source for 7 days (a replay gets 409); the signature covers the id, timestamp and body.
- **Parameter injection:** event fields that try to choose the agent, principal, ceiling, operations, amounts or destination. Answered by: fields fill only declared, typed, bounded parameters in templatable fields; nothing in an event selects identity, authority or step kinds; a field that fails its spec refuses the trigger (HR-276).
- **Repudiation:** every accepted request is audited with source, type, `webhook-id` and body digest; bodies are never stored.
- **Information disclosure:** responses are fixed status codes; nothing says whether an automation matched.
- **Denial of service:** bodies at most 64 KiB; a per-IP limit before verification and a per-source limit after it; one execution per dedupe key (HR-277); hourly execution caps and loop refusal (HR-278).
- **Elevation:** a valid event can at most start an execution of a version people enabled, under the ceiling they approved, with per-grant and per-day caps (R-34).

*Execution authority* (D7–D10). The engine acts as the automation's own service account, which can hold no keys or role bindings (HR-270). A grant it issues is bounded by the ceiling, the envelopes and the promotion gate, lasts at most 24 hours and counts against per-day caps (HR-271). Every start and every effectful step re-checks that the confirmers still hold the permissions they approved with; a lost permission voids the ceiling and pauses the automation (HR-273). A compromised runner agent can act only within the grant issued for its run (R-33).

10. **Enabling (D7, HR-272).**
    - `RequestEnable(version, rollout, cohort, window)` needs `automation.manage`. SHADOW needs a VALIDATED or TESTED version; COHORT and FULL need TESTED (D34). Cohort agents are a subset of the ceiling's agents.

    - An eligible confirmer is an active, non-compromised person holding `automation.publish` and every D3 confirmer permission of every kind the version uses at every scope it can touch.
    - Required: 1 for SHADOW; for COHORT and FULL, 2 when the org has at least two eligible people at request time, else 1 (stored on the request).

A SHADOW enable creates no ceiling.

12. **Ceiling-bounded issuance (D9, F1, F701–F709, HR-271; amends G0 M4 decision 7).**

The `Issue` RPC stays human only.
    - `IssueForAutomationTx`, in order: (1) the ceiling is ACTIVE, before `review_by`, and its version is the enabled one; (2) `CeilingChecker`: every approver is active, still holds `automation.publish`, `grant.issue` and `run.start` at the agent's path and is not compromised; otherwise the ceiling becomes VOID, the automation pauses (CEILING_VOID) and an owned failure goes out; (3) the agent is in the ceiling and grantable; (4) the principal is the version's and active; (5) the grant: grantor the service account, basis `automation <name> ceiling <id>`, expiry at most min(TTL, per-grant TTL, 24 hours), the ceiling's requirements and delegation; (6) the envelopes plus the ceiling as one more envelope, through the normal issue validation; (7) the promotion gate for a PRODUCTION environment (D39); (8) conditional increments of the per-day grant and budget counters (`USAGE_EXCEEDED`); (9) insert with lineage and audit `grant.issued` naming automation, version, execution, ceiling and approvers.     - A person's `Issue` and widening `Revise` also call the promotion gate.

Restore resumes nothing. A containment control on the automation's service account makes revalidation fail and pauses the automation (CONTAINED).

*Triggers (W2)*

16. **The trigger log (D16).** `pc.automation_triggers` records each trigger: kind, mode, source reference, authenticity and its detail (source and secret generation; person and credential; ledger seq; schedule occurrence), parameters (≤ 4 KiB), subjects, root, ancestry (≤ 8), depth (0..3), state (ACCEPTED, SHADOW, QUEUED, DISCARDED, EXPIRED, REFUSED_DUPLICATE, REFUSED_LOOP, REFUSED_LIMIT, REFUSED_CONCURRENCY, REFUSED_INVALID, REFUSED_CONDITION, REFUSED_WINDOW), reason and execution. `pc.automation_dedupe` holds keys with expiry. 17. **Schedules (D17, F672–F674).** `pc.automation_schedules` (cron, timezone, next and last fire) materialized on activation. An in-house cron parser (no new dependency) with `time/tzdata` embedded. A gap time is skipped (default) or shifted to the first valid instant; a repeated time fires once. A dispatcher every 15 seconds claims due rows per org (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, 100 at a time); an occurrence more than 2 minutes late is missed: SKIP records it DISCARDED (MISSED), CATCH_UP fires at most one occurrence within the window. Dedupe `schedule:<automation>:<fire time>`; FORBID overlap with an active execution → REFUSED_CONCURRENCY. `PreviewSchedule(cron, timezone, from, count ≤ 50)` shows local and UTC times with DST notes.
18. **Platform events (D18).** A per-org consumer cursor advanced by compare-and-set, every 5 seconds, at most 20 batches of 500. A closed vocabulary mapped to feed kinds: action.decided, action.held, action.denied, effect.recorded, run.ended, grant.issued, grant.revoked, coverage.lost, alert.raised, incident.opened, case.closed, agent.discovered, approval.expired, automation.finished. `where` is cost-limited CEL that fails closed. Dedupe `event:<ledger entry id>`. Test events never trigger LIVE executions.
19. **Conditions and thresholds (D19).** Closed probes: grant.expiring, approval.waiting, coverage.below, run.long_running, agent.unclaimed, evaluated per org every 60 seconds, firing once per episode when true for at least `for`, at most 1 000 subjects per probe per evaluation (overflow → owned failure). Thresholds count per minute from the feed consumer and fire when the window sum reaches `at_least`, deduped per window bucket, at most 1 000 groups per automation (overflow → owned failure), pruned after 24 hours.
20. **Manual starts (D20).** `StartExecution(automation, params, idempotency_key, step_up)` needs `automation.run`, a person, and a step-up when the version requires it; dedupe `manual:<idempotency key>`.
21. **Inbound event sources (D21, F2, HR-275, HR-276).**
    - `pc.automation_event_sources` (name, types (1..20), secret sealed with the KEK, previous secret and its end, rate 1..600 per minute (default 60), state PENDING | ACTIVE | DISABLED). PENDING on insert; DISABLED is final.

Order: per-IP limit (240 a minute) → 64 KiB cap → Standard Webhooks headers → ACTIVE source (else a uniform 404) → signature in constant time, current or previous secret within the overlap (else 401) → timestamp within ±5 minutes (else 401) → per-source rate (else 429) → `webhook-id` unseen for 7 days in `pc.automation_event_receipts` (else 409) → JSON with a `type` the source declares (else 422) → audit `automation.event_received` (source, type, id, body digest; never the body) → matching INBOUND triggers of ACTIVE automations with parameters extracted per spec (a failure refuses that automation's trigger) → 202. Bodies are never stored; a janitor prunes expired receipts.
22. **Automation destinations and delivery (D22).** `pc.automation_destinations` (URL, internal flag, purposes LAUNCH | DIGEST | NOTICE, secret and previous secret, state PENDING | ACTIVE | PAUSED | DISABLED) with the same lifecycle and RPCs as sources; the URL is checked with the SIEM rule (metadata hosts always refused, private and loopback only when internal). `pc.automation_deliveries` (≤ 64 KiB body, at most 8 attempts, backoff 30 seconds to 1 hour) sent through the egress client with Standard Webhooks headers (both signatures during an overlap) and a 10-second timeout; a 2xx is DELIVERED, which is the DELIVERY_CONFIRMED evidence. 23. **Dedupe, ancestry, loops, caps and pause policies (D23, HR-277, HR-278).**
    - Dedupe by the D16–D21 key or the definition's `dedupe.key` within its window → REFUSED_DUPLICATE.
    - Ancestry: a feed event caused by an automation (its service account, or a run with an execution id) inherits that execution's ancestry plus the automation, with depth plus one and the same root. An automation already in its ancestry, or depth 4 or more, is refused (REFUSED_LOOP) with one `automation.loop` signal (source automation, group key the root, M10's follow-up), one owned failure per root and a LOOP pause of the refused automation.
    - The hourly cap refuses excess triggers (REFUSED_LIMIT) with one grouped owned failure per hour.

*Engine (W3)*

24. **Executions (D24, HR-273).**
    - `pc.automation_executions` (one per trigger; mode; state PENDING | RUNNING | WAITING | SUCCEEDED | PARTIALLY_COMPLETED | FAILED | CANCELLED | NEEDS_RECONCILIATION; reason; current step; ceiling, service account, principal; root, ancestry, depth; deadline; wake time; cancel request). Terminal states are final, except NEEDS_RECONCILIATION, which moves to a final state. `pc.automation_step_attempts` (step, attempt, kind, state, decision, reason, output ≤ 4 KiB, references, effect, compensation flag); terminal rows are immutable. Reasons are stable codes (AUTHORITY_CHANGED, CEILING_VOID, OWNER_INACTIVE, CONTAINED, KILL_SWITCH, DEADLINE, STEP_FAILED, GATE_DECLINED, GATE_EXPIRED, GATE_NO_APPROVER, LAUNCH_NOT_CLAIMED, OUTSIDE_COHORT, CANCELLED_BY_USER, DESTINATION_INACTIVE, TOOL_QUARANTINED, USAGE_EXCEEDED, NOT_PROMOTED).
    - `Starter.Start` locks the automation, checks it is ACTIVE (a SHADOW rollout gives mode SHADOW) and its concurrency, inserts PENDING and enqueues. A River job advances each execution until a wait or a terminal state (at most 20 transitions per job, in a tenant transaction with `FOR UPDATE`); a per-org sweep every 15 seconds wakes due executions; NEEDS_RECONCILIATION is rechecked every 5 minutes.
    - Revalidation before the first step and before every effectful step: owner active; the automation ACTIVE with this version; the ceiling ACTIVE and its checker; the service account active and not contained; the principal active; the kill switch clear; the edition. A failure ends the execution FAILED with its reason, cleanup and an owned failure to the owner.
    - Cleanup at every terminal state revokes the execution's still-active grants, ends its runs and cancels pending launches and deliveries, recorded as cleanup references. Audits: `automation.execution_started`, `automation.step_finished`, `automation.execution_finished`.
25. **Generic steps (D25).**
    - `launch_run`: agent in the ceiling (outside a COHORT rollout's cohort → SKIPPED OUTSIDE_COHORT), task reference, TTL within the per-grant TTL, grant operations within the ceiling and narrowing values, run parameters, delivery PULL or WEBHOOK (a LAUNCH destination), a claim deadline (≤ 1 hour, default 15 minutes) and runner calls (D33). One transaction issues the grant, starts the run and records the launch and its delivery. It waits until claimed; at the claim deadline it fails LAUNCH_NOT_CLAIMED and the grant is revoked; a retry before binding issues a fresh grant.
    - `wait_run` (done when the run ends or the launch completes); `wait_effect` (F500: counts the run's transactions for an operation in the ceiling reaching CONFIRMED or the stated M7 effect states; UNKNOWN, CONFLICTING or UNVERIFIABLE at timeout give UNKNOWN, and for an irreversible operation the execution goes to NEEDS_RECONCILIATION; NONE_CONFIRMED fails).
    - `gate`: approvers by role or user minus exclusions (OWNER by default; CONFIRMERS, PRINCIPAL), 1..2 approvals, deadline ≤ 72 hours; an approval request and a waitlist entry of kind AUTOMATION_GATE, bound to the SHA-256 of `{execution, step, attempt, params digest, summary}`; decided by holders of `automation.approve` on the existing approval page with a security key, routed, reminded and escalated like restoration requests. Declined, expired or no eligible approver fail it.
    - `revoke_grant`, `notify` (`automation.notice` to people, or a NOTICE destination; in SHADOW and TEST the owner only, labelled shadow), `deliver` (done when DELIVERED), `wait` (≤ 24 hours).
    - Shadow (HR-288): in SHADOW and TEST mode every effectful kind returns SHADOWED through `Preview` (`launch_run` uses dry-run issuance and run start); nothing outside the execution's own rows is written.
26. **Launches (D26).** `pc.automation_launches` (agent, optional instance, run, grant, delivery, state PENDING | CLAIMED | COMPLETED | EXPIRED | CANCELLED, claim deadline, outcome, report ≤ 16 KiB, calls, run parameters). `AutomationLaunchService` is workload-authenticated: `ClaimLaunch` returns the caller agent's oldest PENDING launch (instance-pinned launches only for that instance) and `CompleteLaunch` records the outcome and per-call results, by the claiming instance only. A WEBHOOK launch body carries ids, the task reference and run parameters, never secrets, and counts as claimed when the run binds.
27. **Failure handling (D27, F723–F728, HR-280, HR-282).** Only retry-safe kinds retry, with a stable step id, at most 5 attempts and backoff inside the deadline. `on_failure` STOP, CONTINUE or ESCALATE (owner notification and an owned failure, then STOP). Declared compensation runs after STOP, flagged as compensation, and is never reported as an undo. Final state: an UNKNOWN effect of an irreversible operation → NEEDS_RECONCILIATION (resolved when M7 reconciliation resolves its transactions); else every finish condition met → SUCCEEDED; else any CHANGED effect → PARTIALLY_COMPLETED; else a cancel → CANCELLED; else FAILED. The result itemizes changed, unchanged and unknown steps and lists unresolved work.
28. **Controls (D28, F729–F733, F737, HR-283).** Pause (`automation.manage`; triggers follow the paused policy; active executions continue unless `cancel_active`); Resume (revalidates owner, ceiling and checker, identity, principal, edition and kill switch, then replays queued triggers; never replays missed irreversible work); Retire (cancels active executions, deactivates triggers, revokes the ceiling, disables the identity; final); RevokeAuthority (ceiling REVOKED, every grant under it revoked, runs ended, automation PAUSED AUTHORITY_REVOKED); CancelExecution and CancelActiveExecutions (`automation.manage`; CANCELLED at the next boundary with cleanup). Every control returns its verified effects.
29. **Inspector and results (D29, F715–F722, F222).** `GetExecution` returns the execution, its trigger (kind, authenticity, source, parameters, ancestry), version (number, digest, summary), automation, identity, principal, ceiling (approvers, review date), grants with budget use, runs, launches, steps and attempts, gates, deliveries and the result with unresolved work. `ListExecutions` filters transaction details by the viewer's agent scope; `LookupExecution` finds an execution from a run, grant or transaction.
30. **Evidence (D30, F533).** A pack scope `automation_execution` holds the execution (trigger, version and digest with the ceremony confirmations, steps and attempts, result), its grants, runs, the runs' transactions with receipts and effect receipts, and its approvals. Completion statements reference that evidence.

42. **Protected changes (D42, F582–F593, F599, HR-292).** Per-org settings per category (TOOL_MEANING, CONSEQUENCE_RULES, CUSTODY, COVERAGE_EXCLUSION), OFF by default or DISTINCT_PUBLISHER. 43. **The compromised publisher (D43, F595, F596, HR-293; Business).** `MarkCompromised(user, since, reason)` (`publisher.compromise`, one person with a step-up; narrowing) in one transaction blocks that person from confirming changes (both ceremonies), approving, promoting and activating packages; voids their pending change requests; pauses the automations whose ceiling they approved (COMPROMISED_PUBLISHER); and halts open policy rollouts they confirmed. `ListExposed` lists what they published since `since`: policy versions, package activations, org package keys (recommending revocation, F596), automation versions, protected changes, promotions and grants. Another person reviews each item KEEP or ROLLBACK (rollback uses the existing paths). Clearing is a change request (domain `publisher`) needing two other people, allowed only once every item is reviewed. A valid signature never exempts an item.

*Search (W8)*

44. **Entities (D44).** Alerts, incidents, cases, findings, automations and executions, policy versions, packages and gateways join search, each with typed filters and its own read permission.
45. **Quick find (D45, F304, F320, HR-295).** `Find(text 2..100, kinds, limit ≤ 10 per kind)`: an exact UUID, a UUID prefix of at least 8 hex digits, or a case-insensitive prefix or substring on names and titles (people only with member read permission), with escaped `ILIKE`. Each kind is filtered by the viewer's permission and scope before counting; hidden records never contribute to matches, counts or suggestions, and omissions say "outside your permissions". Migration 00147 adds `lower(name)` pattern indexes; no `pg_trgm`.
46. **Plain-language questions (D46, F5, F305, F321, HR-295).** `Interpret(question ≤ 300, timezone)` returns a query or a built-in, the interpretation (entity, readable filters, environment, range with timezone, event meaning ATTEMPTS | CONFIRMED | DECISIONS | EFFECTS, matched phrases), unknown words, ambiguities and whether clarification is needed; `Ask` interprets and runs (no results while clarification is needed). A deterministic tokenizer and lexicon covers entities, outcomes, time phrases, environments, names (through viewer-scoped `Find`) and the built-in phrasings. It never guesses; every answer row links its evidence; there is no model and no outbound call.
47. **Incident correlation (D47, F316; Team).** A built-in `incident_correlation(incident or case, days ≤ 30)` compares the incident's runs with others in the window: shared destinations, credentials (inferred from credential versions by time, labelled INFERRED), operation sequences, grant changes and tool changes, as search items with evidence under the viewer's scope.
48. **Actions and the CLI (D48, F318, HR-296).** `OpenCaseFromResults` (Team, `case.manage`; at most 50 selected items, carrying only their evidence) and `ExportResults` (JSON or CSV; `evidence.export` plus each entity's read permission; at most 10 000 rows; audited `search.exported` with the query). `pclaw search find | ask | run | builtin | saved list | save | run | export`.

**Founder decisions (answered 2026-10-15, the recommended option each time; recorded, not re-asked):**

- **F1: grant issuance.** Each execution gets a fresh grant (at most 24 hours) for the agent it launches, only inside a ceiling in the automation version (target agents, operations, resources, environments, per-grant and per-day budget and count caps). People holding `grant.issue` for the whole ceiling approve it with security keys (two when the org has two); it expires at a review date (at most 90 days) and stops if an approver loses the permission. Agents and ordinary API service accounts never issue grants.
- **F2: outside events.** Signed inbound events: an admin adds each source with a security key; a Standard Webhooks signature per source; replayed, stale and oversized requests refused; rate-limited; event fields fill only typed, bounded declared parameters, never identity, principal, scope or trigger. Plus schedules, platform events, state and threshold conditions, and manual starts.
- **F3: tool calls.** Only through enrolled agents: a closed step catalogue; customer agents or the PantherClaw runner agent `pclaw automation runner`; the server never calls tools.
- **F4: scope.** All of M11: automations, plus F600 cohorts, the F333 promotion gate, F582 protected changes beyond policies, the F595 compromised-publisher response (Business), and the F762 packs whose connectors exist (coding-to-production, read-only diagnostics).
- **F5: plain-language search.** A rule-based parser, no AI model, nothing leaves the server; it shows the generated query, range, timezone and event meaning, and lists the words it did not understand.
- **F6: the promotion gate.** New production grants (issued by a person or an automation) cover only operations the agent is promoted for; existing grants keep working until they expire; the dev seed and the quickstart promote their demo agent.

**Residual risks** (added to THREAT_MODEL §8 by this brief):
- **R-33:** a compromised runner agent (or a customer agent an automation launches) acts with the grant issued for its run. Compensating controls: that grant lasts at most 24 hours, sits inside the ceiling people approved, carries the per-grant and per-day caps and is revoked at the execution's end; every call is decided, held and receipted at the gateway like any agent action (HR-271, HR-285); containment and the kill switch apply.
- **R-34:** a leaked inbound source secret lets its holder start executions. Compensating controls: event fields fill only declared, typed, bounded parameters, never identity or authority (HR-276); every execution stays inside the ceiling and its per-day caps; one execution per dedupe key and hourly caps (HR-277, HR-278); rotation with a 24-hour overlap and disabling are immediate; every request is audited.
- **R-35:** two colluding approvers can enable an automation and approve its ceiling. Compensating controls: both must hold every permission the version needs at every scope it touches (HR-272), with distinct security keys (HR-035); the ceiling is bounded and expires within 90 days; enabling is audited and attributable, and the compromised-publisher response (HR-293) pauses everything they approved.
- **R-36:** plain-language questions can be misread: the parser maps words to filters, and a reader may trust an answer to a question it did not ask. Compensating controls: every answer shows the generated query, range, timezone and event meaning, lists unknown words and ambiguities, never guesses, and links its evidence (HR-295).
- **R-37:** the promotion gate's COVERAGE check accepts PARTIAL coverage, so an operation can be promoted while a cooperative route is not enforced. Compensating controls: PARTIAL is shown per operation in readiness; coverage labels stay on every decision and in exposure; promotion is per operation and revocable.
- **R-38:** schedules depend on the server clock and the embedded timezone data: clock skew, a timezone rule change after a release, and DST gaps or repeats can move, skip or merge an occurrence. Compensating controls: the DST gap and repeat behaviour is declared and previewed (SKIP or SHIFT; a repeated time fires once); missed occurrences follow the declared policy and are recorded; dedupe per occurrence; the embedded `time/tzdata` follows the Go toolchain, which is kept current.
