### G0 — M19 — Scale: high availability, hosted per-org gateways and write-side decision speed — 2026-10-29

**Purpose:** PantherClaw runs today as one server, one worker and one Postgres. Several replicas of the API are possible, but some of what they count and cache is per replica:
- The authenticated API's rate limits multiply with the number of replicas (R-58).
- Stream and wait caps multiply in the same way.
- A gateway certificate revoked on one replica can still be accepted on another for up to a second.
- A2A tasks created through one gateway replica are refused on another.

The database is a single host with no failover, and the worker must stay at one replica. A customer can only use PantherClaw-hosted gateways from a static list in the server's configuration. And each decided refund still takes about 17 database transactions.

M19 closes these gaps:
- Limits and caps hold across replicas.
- Every cache on the decision path has a stated staleness bound.
- The server survives a Postgres failover. It fails closed during the failover and never makes a decision without a read-write primary.
- Workers run with two or more replicas.
- A2A ownership is shared between gateway replicas like MCP state.
- PantherClaw can host one gateway process per organization on shared hosts, provisioned from records instead of configuration.
- A busy budget row is retried once inside the Authority, from a fresh snapshot, before the decision answers `BUDGET_BUSY`.

In product terms this is Root ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)): the deployment gets redundancy and hosted gateways without weakening any decision-path guarantee.

*Limits and caches* (S19a; TB4, TB3; D201).
- **Denial of service (T-214):** a credential, session or caller spreads its requests or streams across replicas, so that per-replica limits and caps multiply. Answered by:
  - leased chunks of the shared counter, with the total bounded (HR-490);
  - stream and wait leases counted across replicas (HR-491).
- **Elevation:** a revoked gateway certificate accepted by another replica. Answered by: forgetting through `LISTEN pc_containment`, and otherwise the 1 s cache lifetime (HR-492).
- **Stale decisions:** a cache on the decision path serving data older than the fail-closed bound. Answered by: a stated bound ≤ 2 s or a LISTEN invalidation for every such cache, with one test each (HR-493, ADR-0040).

*Failover and workers* (S19b; TB5; D202).
- **Tampering / repudiation (T-215):** a promotion of an asynchronous standby loses committed revocations, containment changes or budget reservations, or two primaries accept writes (split brain). Answered by:
  - no decision while no read-write primary answers (HR-495);
  - a promotion is detected from the primary's timeline, at start-up and on every new connection; unless synchronous replication was verified at both ends, the server holds or refuses connections until the epoch bump (HR-496, reusing HR-373, HR-379);
  - `synchronous_commit=remote_apply` with one synchronous standby recommended for production, and counted only when verified on the primary;
  - `target_session_attrs=read-write`, so a connection never writes to a standby.
- **Spoofing:** a database host impersonated during failover. Answered by: TLS verify-full per host (HR-497).
- **Double work:** a job run twice by two workers. Answered by: every River job audited and made safe with two or more workers (HR-498).

*Hosted gateways* (S19c; TB3, TB7, TB9; D203).
- **Elevation (T-216):** one organization's gateway process reaching another's on the same host. Answered by:
  - one process per organization, with a certificate naming one organization (HR-499);
  - the isolation profile (HR-501);
  - slugs unique across organizations, so no organization can take a live hostname (HR-499).
- **Spoofing (T-217):** a hijacked host agent, a stolen enrolment token, or one organization's gateway obtaining another's agent-facing hostname. Answered by:
  - the host identity has no organization authority: its RPCs declare `hosting.agent`, which no role grants, and are reachable only through the host-certificate authenticator (HR-500);
  - each hosted gateway's server certificate names exactly its own `<slug>.gw.<domain>`, with the slug taken from the organization's hosted-gateway row, never from the request (HR-499);
  - only the provisioner enrols a hosted gateway, and the server certificate goes only to the identity its token enrolled (HR-499);
  - the token is single use and delivered only through the process's private tmpfs (HR-502);
  - deprovisioning revokes before it stops the process (HR-503).
- **Denial of service (T-218):** a noisy neighbour exhausting a shared host. Answered by: cgroup CPU, memory and pids limits per process, and a plan quota (HR-501).

*Decision speed* (S19d; TB5; D204, D208).
- **Tampering (T-219):** two racing first-of-period refunds overspending a budget, or a retried decision reusing a stale evaluation. Answered by:
  - decision snapshots stay `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY`, with no write moved into them (HR-504);
  - a busy row is retried at most once, with jitter, inside the Authority and from a fresh snapshot, then answers `BUDGET_BUSY` (HR-505).

**Design decisions:**

*S19a — limits and caches (D201)*

1. **API limits (P1, R-58).**
   - Each replica takes leased chunks from `pc.rate_counters` (`Store.Take(n=k)`), with k = max(1, limit/16), and spends them locally.
   - A chunk expires at the end of its window.
   - On a store error, authenticated calls are refused, except the existing safety and allow classes (G0 M13 D27). HR-376 is unchanged.
   - No lock is held across `shared.Take`, so a stalled store delays a safety or allow call by at most one store timeout, not by the queue (D215).
   - R-58 closes.
2. **Per-instance caps (P2–P4).** The operations-stream cap per caller, the containment streams per certificate and the wait handles per instance are counted in migration 00226 `pc.stream_leases`.
   - Columns: org, kind, key, holder, id, `expires_at`.
   - A lease is inserted on open and counted in the same transaction, heartbeated every 10 s, expires after 30 s, and is deleted on close.
   - The wait handles' per-server limit stays local, as it bounds one process.
3. **authCache (P5).** The gateway certificate cache also forgets the entries of a revoked or disabled gateway on `LISTEN pc_containment`, which the containment hub already listens to (`ForgetOnNotify`, D214). A per-org generation counter drops a result read before the forget (D215). Otherwise the 1 s lifetime bounds it.
4. **Inbound limiters (P6–P10).**
   - These stay per replica, documented in R-66: the per-IP pre-authentication limits of the automation receiver, the OAuth and browser sign-in, and the extension web routes.
   - These move to the shared store: the per-transmitter signal limit and the evidence replay limit.
   - The signal count runs after the ACTIVE transmitter row is found, so there is no pre-authentication database write (HR-494). The replay path logs a store error before it refuses (D220).
   - The evidence replay tests keep every assertion (D214, D217). `TestHR197_ReplayIsRateLimitedPerCaller` uses a short configured window on the database clock instead of the fake clock. `TestHR197_ReplayDecisionThroughTheRPC` excludes only the `pc.rate_counters` rows with scope `evidence.replay` from its row-count comparison, and also asserts that this counter went up.
5. **Caches (P14).** ADR-0040 states each cache's maximum staleness. On the decision path it is ≤ 2 s, or the cache is invalidated through LISTEN. Each cache has one test, in new `*_hr493_test.go` files where the cache's package belongs to another stream (D214).

*S19b — failover, workers, A2A (D202)*

6. **Database connections.**
   - `db.Config` takes several hosts, with `target_session_attrs=read-write` and TLS verify-full for each host.
   - A stable endpoint (a managed service or a proxy) is documented as the alternative.
7. **During failover:**
   - no decisions (`CANNOT_AUTHORIZE` / 503);
   - `/readyz` is false;
   - LISTEN reconnects and re-polls;
   - nothing is served read-only (G11).
8. **Replication stance and promotion detection** (D207, D213; HR-496, R-67).
   - **Configuration.** S19b appends to `internal/server/config.go`: the database `hosts` list and `replication` (`async` or `sync`).
   - **Effective replication.** Replication counts as sync only when the configuration declares sync AND a start-up check on the primary confirms `synchronous_commit = remote_apply` with a non-empty `synchronous_standby_names`. Otherwise it is async, with a warning.
   - **Timeline record.** Migration 00230 `pc.db_primary_timeline` holds the acknowledged primary timeline and its `sync_verified` (D218).
   - **At start-up:**
     - A timeline that moved from the acknowledged one counts as sync only if it is exactly one past it (D223), the old acknowledged row has `sync_verified = true`, AND the new primary verifies (D218). Otherwise the server starts in the maintenance hold (HR-379).
     - `bump-epochs` records the new timeline when it finishes (HR-373).
     - On the first start, with no row yet, the server records the timeline and does not hold.
     - Before relying on `pg_control_checkpoint()`, S19b confirms on postgres:17 that the non-superuser application role can read the timeline. If it cannot, S19b asks before choosing another source.
   - **At run time:**
     - Every new connection reads its timeline in the pool's after-connect hook, from `pg_walfile_name(pg_current_wal_lsn())`, and compares it with the timeline this process saw at start.
     - A connection on a different timeline is accepted only if that timeline is exactly one past the acknowledged one (D223), the acknowledged row has `sync_verified = true`, never on this process's own start-up result alone, AND the new primary verifies sync on that connection (D221). Any jump holds. The new timeline is then recorded. A process in the hold carries no sync.
     - Otherwise the result is `ErrTimelineChanged`. The process refuses every connection and makes no decision, `/readyz` turns false, and liveness fails too, so the orchestrator restarts the process into the start-up hold. The event is logged, and audited when the database allows.
     - After `ErrTimelineChanged`, pooled connections serve nothing: a pgxpool BeforeAcquire hook refuses while the pool is broken, idle connections are closed, and beginning a transaction refuses (D218).
     - Each failover query is one constant, and the request's context goes through the ownership checks, never `context.Background()` (D218).
   - **Runbook.** After an asynchronous promotion, every replica restarts into the hold until `bump-epochs`.
   - **Tests:**
     - an asynchronous promotion holds at start;
     - a declared but unverified sync holds;
     - a verified sync does not hold;
     - a new primary configured sync after an old timeline that was not verified holds (D218);
     - the first start records the timeline;
     - an asynchronous promotion while running refuses and is not ready;
     - a sync promotion with both ends verified reconnects within 2 s;
     - a sync promotion to an unverified primary refuses;
     - a pooled connection opened before the change cannot run a decision after it (D218);
     - T1 unverified, then T2 hold, then T3 stays held (D221).
9. **Workers.**
   - Audit every River job for safety with two workers, and fix those that are not safe (listed first, each in its own package).
   - Then default the Helm chart to `worker.replicas: 2` and add a worker PodDisruptionBudget.
10. **A2A ownership** (D211).
    - Migration 00227 adds the kinds `A2A_TASK` and `A2A_CONTEXT` to `pc.gateway_mcp_state`, with M15's binding and caps (HR-411, HR-412). In `gateways.proto` they are `MCP_STATE_KIND_A2A_TASK = 5` and `MCP_STATE_KIND_A2A_CONTEXT = 6`.
    - S19b only appends enum values and switch cases in `gatewaysrpc/mcpstate.go`, `gateways/app/mcpstate.go` and the per-kind cap queries.
    - Both kinds use the TASK caps and the 1-hour lifetime.
    - An `A2A_TASK` body is the sealed context id. An `A2A_CONTEXT` entry has no body.
    - An entry is reserved before the call goes to the target, so a cap refusal comes before the target creates anything. The cap is keyed by the calling principal, not a null instance id, and every refusal is logged (D218).
    - The reservation uses a per-request id (`ids.NewV7`) and is skipped when `Message.TaskID` is set (D221).
    - Tests per kind: a cross-org or cross-gateway read is refused, and each cap holds.
    - A2A client and drift state, and the per-connection circuit breaker, stay per replica and are documented.
11. **Local failover test.**
    - A compose profile `failover`: two pinned `postgres:17` containers with a synchronous standby, on ports 5434/5435.
    - `TestFailover_*` lives in a new `test/e2e/m19_failover_test.go` with the build tag `integration && failover`, so normal runs and CI never include it (D216). It reuses `start()` and edits no shared file. `test/failover` keeps the HR-498 guard and the helpers.
    - `task failover` brings the profile up, runs `go test -tags "integration failover" -run TestFailover ./test/e2e/`, and always tears it down, under the heavy-run lock.
    - The test checks:
      1. steady refunds through two gateway replicas;
      2. the primary killed;
      3. fail-closed answers during the gap;
      4. the standby promoted;
      5. reconnection, LISTEN resumed, containment within 2 s, the River leader re-elected, and no overspend or double execution;
      6. the asynchronous variant starts in the maintenance hold.
    - It is not a CI gate. The result is recorded in the exit pull request.

*S19c — hosted gateways (D203, G9)*

12. **Records** (D203, D209, D222, D228).
    - Migration 00228 `pc.hosting_hosts`: operator-level, with no `org_id`. Its RLS policy admits only the global purpose `hosting`, like `pc.rate_counters` (00170).
    - Migration 00229 `pc.hosted_gateways`: with `org_id` and RLS, holding the host, state, `gateway_id`, `enrollment_id` and resources.
    - Migration 00229 also adds `pc.hosting_slugs`: operator-level, with the slug as primary key, carrying `org_id` and `hosted_id` (D228), reachable only through the global purpose `hosting`. `<slug>.gw.<domain>` is a public name, so a slug is globally unique, and a slug existing is not a secret.
    - There is no unique index on the tenant table (HR-050). The claim commits in its own global transaction before the tenant transaction, so a crash leaves a slug held, never a record without its claim. `Stopped` works in the same order, and a retry after a lost release still releases. A conflict gives `SLUG_TAKEN`.
    - Org deletion deprovisions the organization's hosted gateways and releases its slugs (D228). `pc_deleter` removes the tenant records through its grant on `pc.hosted_gateways`, and `pc_app` frees the slugs, because `pc_deleter` touches no global table (HR-391).
    - The host agent removes any container with no assignment, since nothing else stops a process whose record is gone (D228).
    - Placement takes `FOR UPDATE` on the host row, which also closes the capacity race (D219).
    - S19c appends three things to `internal/platform/db/tx.go`, which S19b owns: `GlobalHosting GlobalPurpose = "hosting"`, its `globalPurposes` entry, and `ListerParams.HostID`.
    - 00229 redefines `pc.cross_org_list` with the purposes `hosting_due` and `hosting_assigned`. Per-org work runs in tenant transactions, and no other SECURITY DEFINER function is added (HR-053).
    - S19c adds the guard test `TestCrossOrgListAcceptsEveryPurpose` if none exists. It checks that the function accepts every Go lister purpose constant after all migrations (E6).
13. **Provisioning.**
    - A worker job (the provisioner) creates the gateway and a single-use enrolment token.
    - The host agent `cmd/pantherclaw-host` pulls the token over its own mTLS identity: a new host certificate kind with no organization and the purpose `hosting`, which can never call organization RPCs.
    - It hands the token to the gateway process through a file on that process's private tmpfs, never an environment variable or a log. The gateway reads it with the append-only flag `--hosted-token-file` (`internal/gateway/cli.go`).
    - **Permission** (D212). The host agent's RPCs (`HostingAgentService`) declare the new permission `hosting.agent`.
      - It is declarable only, like `gateway.sync`: no role grants it.
      - It lives in a new `internal/tenancy/domain/permissions_m19_hosting.go`, or as one line in `permissions.go` if the declarable list cannot be appended from there.
      - `wiring_m19_hosting.go` mounts the service with the host-certificate authenticator only, never the gateway or user one, and registers the provisioner jobs.
    - **Org-facing RPCs.** The hosted-gateway RPCs for organizations reuse `gateway.manage` and `gateway.read`.
    - **Enrolment** (D219, D222). `CreateEnrollmentToken` refuses a gateway that a `hosted_gateways` row names, so only the provisioner enrols it. A reissued token replaces `hosted_gateways.enrollment_id`, and Enroll refuses a hosted gateway's token unless its id equals that value. No grant on `expires_at` is added (HR-180).
14. **Agent-facing TLS** (D210).
    - The organization's container generates its own key in its own volume, and the key never leaves it.
    - Over its own mTLS identity it gets a 7-day server certificate from the internal gateway CA, through a new `IssueHostedServer` in `internal/gateways/ca/host.go`.
    - The CA puts exactly one DNS name on the certificate, `<slug>.gw.<domain>`. It takes the slug from the organization's hosted-gateway row, never from the request, and issues only to the identity the provisioner's token enrolled, as recorded on the hosted row (D219).
    - Renewal starts at two-thirds of the lifetime. There is never a wildcard, and no key is shared across organizations. Agents pin the CA as they do today.
    - Tests: organization A's gateway cannot get organization B's hostname, an unknown slug is refused, and an organization admin with `gateway.manage` cannot get a server certificate for its hosted gateway.
15. **Isolation.**
    - One container per organization: a non-root uid, a read-only root, all capabilities dropped, no Docker socket, cgroup CPU, memory and pids limits, and its own network namespace.
    - It is started through the Docker CLI with fixed arguments (the HR-216 pattern).
    - No connector containers (HR-084 unchanged).
16. **Routing.** A per-org hostname `<slug>.gw.<domain>` with SNI passthrough, so the gateway's mTLS stays end to end.
17. **Plans** (D212).
    - Team plan and above, one hosted gateway per organization, with a plan quota.
    - The `HostedGateways` gate, appended to `licence.go`, covers provisioning only (D161's semantics).
    - After a downgrade, an existing hosted gateway keeps running, so agents see no outage.
    - Deprovisioning and reads work in every edition.
18. **Upgrades.** The host agent rolls one organization at a time, with a health check, toward the target version of M18's fleet policy.
19. **Deprovisioning.** The host agent revokes the certificate (epoch) before it stops the process.
20. **Fallback.** The configuration list `gateways.pantherclaw_hosted` stays as a fallback for one release.

**Decisions during the build (D223–D230):**

- **D223, timeline steps.** `decidePromotion` and `acknowledge` accept only a timeline exactly one past the acknowledged one; any jump holds (HR-496).
- **D224, reconnects.** The containment hub's backoff is capped at 500 ms and resets after a successful LISTEN. On reconnect every org poller wakes, and the whole gateway certificate cache is forgotten, because a missed notification could be for any org. The 2 s bound stays. `acknowledge` may accept `tl == ack` on a verified row.
- **D225, the exit.** `feat/m19-exit` was built from `main` at exact stream heads, keeping both sides of every conflict, with a new `test/e2e/m19_exit_e2e_test.go` and the trace default at M19. `task failover` was the landing gate: any miss of the 2 s bound blocks landing until its cause is proven.
- **D226, hosted open items.** `hosting add-host` was added, with its section in [gateway.md](../runbooks/gateway.md). Team and Business get no roll-out. The same decision's acceptance of a deleted org keeping its slug was reversed by D228.
- **D227, a stuck read.** The 2 s miss came from a containment read with no deadline, blocked on one stuck connection attempt. Each read now has a deadline of about 500 ms. `connect_timeout` stays 5 s: a gateway still refuses at 2 s because `Check()` is local, so a silent old primary delays delivery but cannot fail open ([backup-restore.md](../runbooks/backup-restore.md) §8).
- **D228, slugs and deletion.** See decision 12. 00229 was edited in place, since M19 had not landed.
- **D229, `TestT219`.** It passed with the retry removed, so it now waits for the real lock wait in `pg_stat_activity`, releases about 110 ms later, and asserts exactly one `ErrBusy`. It fails with the retry removed.
- **D230, late review.** The six newer River jobs were audited for two workers before they were listed. The exit test's budget leg uses D229's technique, and its limits leg uses a scope no real traffic shares. The runbook states that a read over 500 ms fails closed. Test-only changes keep the failover gate valid; a production change needs two more runs.
